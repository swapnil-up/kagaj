Thereafter, the Right Honourable Speaker presented the following proposal for decision in accordance with Rule 180 of the House of Representatives Rules, 2083: "Formation of a Parliamentary Special Committee on the Impact of Climate Change in the Himalayas, Climate Justice, and the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli Disaster", which the House unanimously approved:-
1. Background and Rationale
The devastating disaster in the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli river system has severely impacted the lives, housing, infrastructure, energy, transportation, trade, agriculture, and livelihoods of the citizens in the affected areas, not only disrupting but also destroying the Trishuli civilization. Its impact extends beyond the disaster-affected settlements to supply chains, public services, and economic activities. This event highlights the need to prioritize immediate search, rescue, and relief, as well as safe resettlement, sustainable reconstruction, and future risk mitigation at the national level.
The relationship between the impact of climate change in the Himalayan region, the state of glaciers and glacial lakes, changes in Himalayan watersheds, and the security of downstream areas is a matter that needs to be seriously addressed along with the study of this disaster. The immediate cause of the event, the factors exacerbating it, and its relationship with climate change should be identified based on available scientific evidence and research findings. This is necessary for use in early warning systems, settlement development, infrastructure construction, and disaster preparedness.
From the perspective of climate justice, the historical and current responsibility for carbon emissions, the unequal distribution of risks, and the losses and damages suffered by the affected citizens must also be at the center of the discussion. For this, Nepal needs to build a strong national stance based on scientific evidence and pursue strong diplomatic initiatives on climate finance, adaptation, technology, and international cooperation.
Against this background, I propose to form a parliamentary special committee in accordance with Rule 180 of the House of Representatives Rules, 2083, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee which met on Ashoj 5, 2083, to study the impact of climate change in the Himalayan region, climate justice, and the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli disaster, to provide policy recommendations, facilitate inter-agency coordination, and conduct parliamentary oversight.
2. Name and Objectives of the Committee:
(a) The name of the committee shall be the "Parliamentary Special Committee on the Impact of Climate Change in the Himalayas, Climate Justice, and the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli Disaster".
(b) The objective of the committee shall be to study the impact of climate change in the Himalayas and the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli disaster and recommend a course of action for the state to adopt for the resettlement of affected citizens, sustainable reconstruction, future risk mitigation, necessary legal and institutional reforms, and climate justice.
3. Formation and Structure of the Committee:
(a) The committee shall consist of 15 members based on subject matter expertise, inclusivity, and representation from disaster-affected areas, as follows:

(b) The committee shall elect a chairperson from among its members at its first meeting.
4. Term of the Committee:
(a) The term of the committee shall be three months from the date of its first meeting.
(b) If the work cannot be completed within the stipulated period, the committee may request the House of Representatives for an extension of the term, stating the work completed, the remaining work, the reasons for the extension, and the necessary additional time, and the term shall be extended as per the decision of the House of Representatives.
4. Terms of Reference of the Committee (TOR):
(a) Study of the Impact of Climate Change in the Himalayas: This includes reviewing available studies and data on Himalayan temperatures, snowfall, glaciers, glacial lakes, and watershed changes and the resulting risks. Identifying gaps in study and monitoring and recommending measures to strengthen scientific research and regular monitoring.
(b) Study of the Causes and Impacts of the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli Disaster: This includes conducting expert and scientific evidence-based studies on the origin of the disaster, its sequence of events, immediate causes, and factors exacerbating the risks. Reviewing the potential relationship with climate change, the impact of transboundary watersheds, and the effects on downstream areas.
(c) Review of Search, Rescue, Relief, and Resettlement: This includes reviewing search and rescue operations, the status of missing persons, relief distribution, safe temporary shelter, basic services, and livelihood restoration for the just addressing of the affected population. Recommending immediate and medium-term corrective measures based on the needs of the affected citizens.
(d) Roadmap for Sustainable and Safe Reconstruction: This includes recommending a roadmap with risk assessment, safe site selection, technical standards, priorities, financial resources, and timelines for the reconstruction of damaged housing, roads, bridges, energy, drinking water, irrigation, and public infrastructure. Monitoring the quality of implementation, transparency, and progress.
(e) Disaster Risk Reduction and Early Warning System: This includes recommending measures for multi-hazard risk mapping, safe settlement development, protection of sensitive infrastructure, and community preparedness. Recommending a framework for strengthening the system from glacier, river, rainfall, and landslide monitoring to community warnings and safe evacuation.
(f) Carbon Emissions, Climate Justice, and Loss and Damage: This includes reviewing available evidence on historical and current responsibility for carbon emissions and the climate risks faced by Nepal. Recommending measures for documenting losses and damages of affected citizens, accessing climate finance, and presenting Nepal's claims and stance in international forums.
(g) Climate Diplomacy and Transboundary Cooperation: This includes recommending measures to strengthen bilateral, regional, and multilateral cooperation for Himalayan conservation, adaptation, financial and technical assistance, joint research, and transboundary risk information exchange.
(h) Legal and Institutional Reforms: This includes reviewing existing laws and their implementation related to disaster management, environment, climate change, land use, infrastructure safety, and protection of affected citizens. Recommending legislative initiatives with basis, objectives, and proposed provisions for necessary amendments or new laws.
(i) Coordination and Parliamentary Oversight: This includes recommending measures to clarify responsibilities, resource mobilization, and implementation coordination among all three tiers of government and concerned agencies. Conducting parliamentary oversight of government decisions, programs, budgets, and progress related to disaster management and reconstruction.
6. Methodology:
(a) The committee may determine its own working procedures.
(b) It shall obtain details from relevant agencies, consult with experts and officials, solicit suggestions from affected citizens, and conduct field studies as needed.
(c) The committee shall maintain its parliamentary autonomy and accountability by engaging in necessary dialogue and coordination with stakeholders and mechanisms.
7. Report and Expected Outcomes:
(a) Within three months, the committee shall submit a report to the House of Representatives that includes study findings, issues requiring immediate attention, a roadmap for sustainable reconstruction, legal and institutional reforms, and priorities for climate justice and diplomacy.
(b) The report shall also specify the responsible agencies, timelines, required resources, and monitoring indicators for the implementation of the recommended actions. It may also submit an interim report or short-term recommendations on issues requiring immediate attention.
