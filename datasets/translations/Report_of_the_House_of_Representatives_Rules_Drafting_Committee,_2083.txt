Federal Parliament
House of Representatives
Report of the Federal Parliament Rules Drafting Committee, 2083

Honorable Speaker,

As per Article 104(1) of the Constitution of Nepal, the House of Representatives, by its meeting on 2082 Chaitra 23, formed a 15-member Rules Drafting Committee to manage its proceedings, maintain order in meetings, and further systematize the formation, work, and proceedings of committees and the House. The proposal was presented by Honorable Kabindra Bullakoti and supported by Honorable Sita Thapaliya, Honorable Ganesh Singh Thagura, Honorable Yubaraj Dulal, Honorable Dhurbaraj Rai, Honorable Bharat Giri, and Honorable Pushpa Kumari Chaudhary. The proposal was unanimously passed by the House.

The committee was tasked with preparing a draft of the House of Representatives Rules to regulate its proceedings, maintain order in meetings, and manage administrative functions in accordance with the Constitution of Nepal, and to complete all tasks necessary for the passage of the rules.

Furthermore, the committee was given a working period of 14 days from the date of commencement of its work. It was also arranged that the committee could manage its own procedures under the general direction of the Speaker. On Chaitra 24, after discussing the procedures to be adopted during the drafting process, the committee secretariat was instructed to draft the procedural guidelines. The first meeting of the committee was held on Chaitra 25 under the chairmanship of Honorable Ganesh Parajuli, where the draft and internal procedures prepared by the secretariat were discussed and passed with amendments. On the same day, the committee began the work of identifying subjects for improvement in the House of Representatives Rules, 2079, and preparing a draft.

Subsequently, the committee's meetings were held continuously, generally from 8:30 AM, focusing on the drafting of the rules, and considering the Constitution of Nepal, prevailing laws, and past House of Representatives rules. The committee held meetings on Chaitra 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, and 2083 Baisakh 2, 3, 4, 6, and 7 to prepare the draft of the rules.

Suggestions made by the members of the committee were prioritized and incorporated into the draft rules after internal discussions within their respective parties.

On Baisakh 6, 2083, the committee meeting invited former Secretaries General of the Federal Parliament Secretariat, Mr. Manohar Prasad Bhattarai and Dr. Bharatraj Gautam, as experts. They provided suggestions regarding past practices, experiences, international trends, and the strengths and weaknesses of the rules in conducting parliamentary proceedings. On the same day, the committee specially invited the Honorable Speaker to brief him on the draft rules and present the main suggestions made for improving the prevailing rules.

The committee, after discussing the provisions of the rules, prepared the draft of the rules. The draft, with necessary amendments, was passed by the committee meeting held on Baisakh 8, 2083. The internal procedures of the committee, the proposal regarding the formation of the committee, and the details of the staff working in the committee secretariat are mentioned in the annexes.

Finally, I express my sincere gratitude to the Honorable Speaker, the Honorable Members of the committee, the Secretary of the House of Representatives, the subject matter experts, the Secretary of the committee, and all the staff of the committee secretariat for their diligent work in drafting the House of Representatives Rules. I present the House of Representatives Rules Draft, 2083, to the esteemed House.

(Ganesh Parajuli)
Chairperson
House of Representatives Rules Drafting Committee
Baisakh 8, 2083, Monday

House of Representatives Rules, 2083

Preamble:
This regulation has been made by exercising the authority granted by Article 104(1) of the Constitution of Nepal to regulate the proceedings of the House of Representatives, maintain order in meetings, regulate the formation, work, and proceedings of committees and the House, and to resolve any obstacles that may arise.

Chapter 1
Preliminary

1. Short Title and Commencement: (1) This regulation shall be called the "House of Representatives Rules, 2083".
(2) This regulation shall come into force immediately.

2. Definitions: Unless the subject or context otherwise requires, in this regulation:-
(a) "Finance Minister" shall mean the Finance Minister of the Government of Nepal, and shall also include any other minister or state minister designated to perform the functions of the Finance Minister.
(b) "Presiding Member" shall mean the Speaker and the Deputy Speaker, and in their absence, a member nominated according to Rule 10 to preside over the meeting.
(c) "Unparliamentary Word" shall mean any word that is indecent, obscene, insulting, objectionable, offensive, contrary to public decency or morality, demeaning to any person, caste, religion, language, or gender, discriminatory in any other way, reducing dignity, making unverified accusations, or reflecting geographical discrimination.
(d) "Deputy Speaker" shall mean the Deputy Speaker of the House of Representatives.
(e) "Senior Member" shall mean the senior member as per Article 91(5) of the Constitution in the event of the election of the Speaker and Deputy Speaker not taking place or both positions being vacant.
(f) "Table" shall mean the table of the House used to inform the House about a proposal presented by a member after discussion, along with related documents, and shall also include the act of presenting any document or information to the House.
(g) "Official" shall mean the Speaker, Deputy Speaker, Leader of the Parliamentary Party of a political party represented in the House of Representatives, Deputy Leader of the Parliamentary Party of a political party represented in the House of Representatives, Chief Whip, Whip, Whip of the ruling party, Whip, and Chairperson.
(h) "Premises" shall mean the entire area of the House of Representatives, including the meeting hall and ante-chambers, and shall also include any place designated as premises from time to time by the Speaker.
(i) "House of Representatives" shall mean the House of Representatives as per Article 84 of the Constitution.
(j) "Delegated Legislation" shall mean rules, orders, regulations, directives, procedures, or standards.
(k) "Proposal" shall mean any proposal presented for consideration in the House of Representatives.
(l) "Presenting Member" shall mean a minister in relation to a government bill, the proponent of a non-governmental bill, or the proposer of any other proposal under this regulation.
(m) "Meeting Hall" shall mean the meeting hall of the House of Representatives, and shall also include the adjoining balcony and spectator stands.
(n) "Minister" shall mean a minister of the Government of Nepal, including a Prime Minister, Deputy Prime Minister, Minister, State Minister, or Assistant Minister.
(o) "Council of Ministers" shall mean the Council of Ministers of the Government of Nepal as per Article 76 of the Constitution.
(p) "Security Personnel" shall mean the security personnel deployed to maintain order and security in the House or committee.
(q) "Secretary General" shall mean the Secretary General of the Federal Parliament appointed as per Article 106(1) of the Constitution, and shall also include a Secretary or Secretariat Secretary designated by the Speaker in consultation with the Chairperson of the National Assembly to perform the functions of the Secretary General in his absence.
(r) "Secretary" shall mean the Secretary of the House of Representatives appointed as per Article 106(1) of the Constitution, and shall also include a Secretariat Secretary or senior officer designated by the Speaker to perform the functions of the Secretary of the House of Representatives in his absence.
(s) "Secretariat" shall mean the Federal Parliament Secretariat as per Article 907 of the Constitution.
(t) "House", "Assembly", or "Meeting" shall mean the House, Assembly, or Meeting of the House of Representatives.
(u) "Member" shall mean a member of the House of Representatives, and shall also include a minister presenting a proposal.
(v) "Treaty" shall mean a treaty or agreement to which the State of Nepal or the Government of Nepal is a party.
(w) "Chairperson" shall mean the chairperson of a committee.
(x) "Speaker" shall mean the Speaker of the House of Representatives.
(y) "Committee" shall mean a committee formed under this regulation.
(z) "Notice Paper" shall mean the notice paper of the House of Representatives containing the following matters:-
(1) Notice regarding the proceedings of the meeting,
(2) Brief record of the proceedings of the meeting,
(3) Notice related to committees,
(4) Any other matter deemed necessary by the Speaker.
(aa) "Constitution" shall mean the Constitution of Nepal.
(bb) "Minister of Parliamentary Affairs" shall mean the minister responsible for parliamentary affairs of the Government of Nepal.

Chapter 2
Conduct of Sessions and Meetings of the House of Representatives

3. Summoning and Prorogation of Session: (1) The notice of the summoning of a session by the President as per Article 93(1) of the Constitution shall be given to the members by the Secretary General or, in his absence, by the Secretary.
(2) If the session summoned as per Sub-rule (1) is prorogued by the President as per Article 93(2) of the Constitution, the Speaker shall read out the notice in the meeting. However, if the session is prorogued while the meeting of the House of Representatives is adjourned, the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall inform the members through the notice paper.
(3) If one-fourth of the total number of members of the House of Representatives requests the President in writing, as per Article 93(3) of the Constitution, to convene a session or meeting when the session is not in session or is adjourned, the President shall specify the date and time for such session or meeting.
(4) The President shall publish the notice of the summoning and prorogation of the session of the House of Representatives as per Article 93 in the Gazette and, as needed, through public information and mass media.

4. Member Attendance: (1) Before taking their seats, each member shall mark their attendance in the attendance register or electronic attendance device kept outside the meeting hall or in the ante-chamber.
(2) The Secretariat may publicly disclose information regarding member attendance through electronic media.

5. Seating Arrangement: (1) Members shall take their seats in the order and place determined by the Speaker in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.
(2) In the case of members with disabilities, the Speaker may designate a specific place outside the hall and permit one accompanying person to sit.

6. Conduct and Adjournment of Meeting: (1) The meeting of the House of Representatives shall be held on the day and time specified by the Speaker, keeping in mind the workload, subject to this regulation.
(2) The Speaker shall generally commence the meeting at 1:00 PM. However, if the meeting cannot commence even after two hours from the scheduled time, the Speaker shall adjourn the meeting and announce the next meeting.
(3) If the day, time, or agenda of the meeting is changed for any reason, a notice shall be posted on the notice board with reasons, and information shall also be provided to the members through electronic media. Such information shall be deemed to have been received by all members. The Speaker shall announce the commencement and adjournment of each meeting.
(4) The Speaker shall announce the commencement and adjournment of each meeting. However, if the Speaker is unable to be present due to unavoidable circumstances, the Deputy Speaker shall announce the commencement and adjournment of the meeting, or the adjournment of a meeting commenced by the Speaker.
(5) The presence of a minister shall be mandatory in the meeting, and the concerned minister shall also be mandatory when discussing a subject related to their ministry.
(6) The officials of all political parties represented in the House of Representatives shall cooperate in the conduct of the meeting.

Chapter 3
Election of Speaker and Deputy Speaker and Nomination of Presiding Members

7. Election of Speaker: (1) The election of the Speaker shall be held on the day and time specified by the Senior Member after the general election. The Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall publish the notice.
(2) Within the time specified in the program published as per Sub-rule (1), any member shall submit a proposal to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary, to elect another member as Speaker, in the format of Schedule-1.
(3) After the proposal is presented by the proposing member in the meeting, a maximum of three supporting members may support it. If only one proposal is received, after the supporting members have spoken, the person presiding shall declare the proposed member elected as Speaker unopposed.
(4) If more than one proposal is received as per Sub-rule (2), after the proposing members present their proposals and the supporting members support them in order, a brief discussion shall be held on all proposals, and then the presiding person shall present the proposals to the House for decision one by one.
(5) If more than one proposal is presented as per Sub-rule (2) and presented for decision as per Sub-rule (4), the member proposed in the proposal that is passed by the total majority of the House of Representatives at that time shall be deemed elected as Speaker, and the remaining proposals shall not be presented for decision.
(6) The decision on the proposal as per Sub-rule (5) shall be made by a division of votes, with signatures.
(7) If the proposal as per Sub-rule (5) cannot be passed by the required majority of the House of Representatives, a re-election shall be held.
(8) If the member presiding over the meeting is proposed for the post of Speaker, the next senior member who is not proposed for the post of Speaker shall preside over the meeting for that day.
(9) If the post of Speaker becomes vacant for any reason, the Deputy Speaker shall set the date and time for the election within five days from the date of vacancy if the session is in progress, or from the date of commencement of the session if the session is not in progress. If the post of Deputy Speaker is also vacant, the Senior Member shall set such date and time.

8. Election of Deputy Speaker: (1) The election of the Deputy Speaker shall be held on the day and time specified by the Speaker as provided for in Article 99 of the Constitution. The Secretary shall publish the notice.
(2) The procedure for the election of the Deputy Speaker shall be the same as the procedure determined for the election of the Speaker.

9. Oath: Before assuming office, the Speaker shall take an oath of office and secrecy before the President, and the Deputy Speaker shall take an oath of office and secrecy before the Speaker, in the format of Schedule-2.

10. Nomination of Presiding Members: (1) The Speaker may nominate a maximum of seven members at the beginning of each session, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee, to preside over the meeting in the absence of the Speaker and the Deputy Speaker.
(2) The Deputy Speaker and the nominated member as per Sub-rule (1) shall exercise all the powers that the Speaker can exercise during a meeting presided over by them.

Chapter 4
Conduct of Meetings

11. Days for Government and Non-Government Business: Every Friday when the House meets, the time after question hour shall be prioritized for non-governmental business, and on other days, governmental business shall be prioritized. However, if the House does not meet on a Friday for any reason, the Speaker may designate another working day for such business.

Explanation: For the purpose of this rule, "Governmental Business" shall mean business related to any bill or proposal or subject presented by a minister on behalf of the government, and "Non-Governmental Business" shall mean business related to any bill or proposal or subject presented by a member other than a minister.

12. Daily Agenda: (1) In the absence of the Speaker, the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall prepare a tentative agenda one day before the meeting and provide information to the members and parliamentary party offices through electronic media on the same day.
(2) As directed by the Speaker, the agenda shall be prepared one hour before the meeting and a copy shall be made available to the members.
(3) Unless otherwise provided in this regulation, no matter not included in the agenda for the day shall be considered without the permission of the Speaker.

13. Business Advisory Committee: (1) The Speaker may form a Business Advisory Committee consisting of a maximum of fifteen members, excluding ex-officio members, based on proportional representation of political parties in the House, to systematically manage the meeting, including determining the priority and time limit of matters to be presented in the meeting.
(2) The Deputy Speaker and the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs shall be the ex-officio Vice-Chairperson and member of the committee, respectively.
(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in Sub-rule (1), the Speaker may invite any member deemed appropriate by him to attend the meeting of the Business Advisory Committee as a member.
(4) The committee formed as per Sub-rule (1) shall meet regularly during the session and as needed at other times.

14. Time Allocation: (1) The Speaker shall allocate time for discussion on matters presented in the meeting.
(2) Unless otherwise provided in this regulation, after the allocated time is over, the Speaker shall present all necessary proposals for decision to the House without allowing further discussion.

15. Special Time: (1) The Speaker may allocate Zero Hour and Special Time for members to present matters of contemporary importance that they deem necessary. However, if a member requests time urgently regarding a serious incident or matter that has occurred during the period between the previous meeting and the commencement of the upcoming meeting, the Speaker may grant emergency time.
(2) The concerned minister shall provide a response to the issues raised during the emergency, zero, and special time as per Sub-rule (1) within seven days in the House.

16. Time for Giving Notice: Unless otherwise provided in this regulation, notice shall be given to the Secretary between 11:00 AM and 2:00 PM. Notice given outside this time shall be considered given on the next working day.

17. Amendment of Notice: If any word or phrase in a notice is found to be objectionable, sarcastic, unnecessary, inappropriate, or irrelevant, the Speaker may amend or cause it to be amended before its distribution.

18. Distribution of Documents: (1) The Secretary shall provide necessary documents and information to the members.
(2) The Secretary shall arrange for the distribution of documents and information to be provided to members through the official email or application of the Secretariat. Documents and information provided in this manner shall be deemed to have been received by the concerned member.

19. Commencement of Meeting: After the Speaker arrives in the meeting hall and the national anthem is played, the Speaker shall announce the commencement of the meeting.

20. Rules to be Observed in the Meeting: The following rules shall be observed in the meeting:-
(a) All shall stand and show respect upon entering the meeting hall.
(b) The Speaker shall show respect to the meeting and take their seat.
(c) Members shall not leave the meeting hall until the Speaker adjourns the meeting and leaves the hall.
(d) Members shall speak only after being given permission by the Speaker.
(e) Members participating in the meeting shall address the Speaker while speaking and speak from the designated place unless the Speaker orders otherwise.
(f) No member shall interrupt the Speaker while speaking, nor shall they leave their seat without the Speaker's permission.
(g) No member shall walk between the Speaker's seat and the member speaking.
(h) Members shall not sit or stand with their backs turned towards the Speaker's seat.
(i) No member shall do anything that obstructs the proceedings of the meeting, violates its dignity, or causes disorder.
(j) Matters not directly related to the business of the meeting shall not be discussed.
(k) Mobile phones shall be kept in silent mode in the meeting hall, and phone calls shall not be made. Taking photos and audio/video recording shall not be allowed.

21. Rules to be Observed by Members Participating in Discussions: Members participating in discussions in the meeting shall observe the following rules:-
(a) Stand to draw the Speaker's attention and speak only after their name is called or signaled by the Speaker.
(b) The conduct of the Speaker shall not be criticized, except in the context of a motion regarding the Speaker's conduct.
(c) Matters prohibited from debate by Article 104 of the Constitution shall not be discussed.
(d) No one shall speak indecently, obscenely, insultingly, objectionably, or in a manner contrary to public decency or morality, or in a way that demeans any person, caste, religion, language, or gender, or use unparliamentary words.
(e) The right to speak shall not be misused with the intention of obstructing the business of the House.

22. Order of Speaking in Discussion: The order of speaking and responding in discussions shall be as follows:-
(a) After the proposing member has spoken, members shall speak in the order called or signaled by the Speaker.
(b) Unless otherwise provided in this regulation, no member shall speak more than once on the same proposal without the permission of the Speaker.
(c) The proposing member shall have the right to speak again at the end of the discussion to respond.
(d) A member presenting an amendment to a proposal shall not have the right to respond without the permission of the Speaker.

23. Relevance: (1) What a member says shall be relevant to the matter under immediate consideration by the House.
(2) If a member speaks irrelevantly or repeatedly states their own or others' arguments, the Speaker may draw the attention of the meeting to the statement and urge them to speak on the subject. If they do not speak on the subject, they may be ordered to stop speaking. Upon such an order, the member shall stop speaking and take their seat.

24. Clarification May Be Sought or Given: (1) During a discussion, if any member deems it necessary, they may seek clarification through the Speaker.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in Sub-rule (1), any member may, with the permission of the Speaker, provide clarification on matters related to them for the information of the House. However, no controversial issue shall be raised while giving such clarification.
(3) No further discussion shall take place on the clarification given as per Sub-rule (2).

25. Conclusion of Discussion: (1) Unless otherwise provided in this regulation, the discussion shall be deemed concluded after the proposing member has responded.
(2) If the Speaker deems that the discussion on the main proposal has concluded, they may declare it concluded.
(3) If the discussion is deemed to be unnecessarily prolonged, the Speaker may, after ascertaining the opinion of the House, set a time for the conclusion of the discussion, and upon setting such time, the Speaker shall consider the discussion concluded within that period and present all necessary proposals for decision on the main proposal to the House.

26. Point of Order: (1) If any member feels that any provision of the rules has been violated in relation to a matter under immediate consideration by the House, they may raise a point of order, specifying which rule has been violated.
(2) The member raising the point of order shall speak only after receiving permission from the Speaker and shall stop speaking upon receiving a signal from the Speaker.
(3) The member raising the point of order shall state the basis for it and speak only on the matter of the point of order. After raising the point of order, the member shall take their seat.
(4) The Speaker shall decide on the point of order raised in the meeting, and the decision shall be final.

Chapter 4
Method of Decision Making

27. Decision by Proposal: When the Speaker presents a proposal under consideration in the House for decision, it shall be presented in the form of a question.

28. Method of Presenting for Decision: (1) After the discussion on a proposal is concluded, the Speaker shall present the proposal for decision by asking members in favor to say "Aye" and members against to say "No".
(2) After the proposal is presented for decision as per Sub-rule (1), the Speaker shall announce the decision based on which side has the majority of "Aye" or "No" votes.
(3) If the Speaker's announcement as per Sub-rule (2) is not objected to as per Rule 29, the Speaker's announcement shall be considered the decision of the House of Representatives.
(4) After a proposal has been presented for decision, no amendment or motion for adjournment shall be presented on that proposal.

29. Division: (1) If at least twenty-five members express disagreement with the Speaker's announcement as per Rule 28(2) by standing up from their seats, the Speaker shall collect votes by dividing the members who say "Aye", "No", and "Abstain" in one of the following ways:-
(a) By asking each member to pronounce the words "Aye", "No", and "Abstain", or
(b) By having members who say "Aye", "No", and "Abstain" enter separate lobbies and sign a list in alphabetical order, or
(c) By operating an automatic vote recording machine.
(2) Before ordering a division, the Speaker may determine the decision of the House by asking members to stand up in order from their seats.
(3) If an automatic vote recording machine is used for vote counting as per Sub-rule (1) or (2):-
(a) If the Speaker deems that a member is unable to operate the machine for any reason, the vote may be counted after asking the member.
(b) If the vote count is incorrect due to the wrong button being pressed on the automatic vote recording machine or due to the division, the member may correct their vote after drawing the Speaker's attention before the decision is announced.
(4) The Speaker shall immediately announce the result of the vote count as per Sub-rule (1) or (2), and no one shall object to it.
(5) The Speaker shall have the authority to present any proposal in parts as a question for decision. The Speaker shall also have the authority to determine the method of vote collection and division with signatures.

Chapter 6
Order in the Meeting

30. Warning: The Speaker may warn a member who behaves indecently in the meeting to control their behavior. Such a member shall immediately control their behavior after being warned.

31. Expulsion from Meeting Hall: (1) A member who does not comply with the order as per Rule 30 may be ordered by the Speaker to leave the meeting hall. Upon receiving the order, such a member shall immediately leave the meeting hall and shall not be allowed to attend the remaining meeting for that day.
(2) If such a member does not leave the meeting hall immediately after receiving the order as per Sub-rule (1), the Speaker may have them removed with the help of the security personnel.
(3) A member expelled from the meeting as per Sub-rule (2) shall not be allowed to participate in the meeting of the House or any committee for the next three days. The Secretary shall inform the concerned committee and parliamentary party about such expulsion.

32. Suspension of Member: (1) The Speaker may name a member who disrespects the Speaker's seat, intentionally obstructs the proceedings of the House, behaves indecently, or intentionally violates or disregards the Constitution or this regulation.
(2) If a member is named as per Sub-rule (1), a proposal shall be immediately presented to the House by the Speaker for their suspension. No amendment or adjournment motion shall be presented on such a proposal.
(3) If the proposal as per Sub-rule (2) is passed, such a member shall be deemed suspended and shall not be allowed to attend the meetings of the House or any committee for a maximum of fifteen days thereafter.
(4) A suspended member shall immediately leave the meeting hall as per Sub-rule (3).
(5) If a suspended member does not leave the meeting hall as per Sub-rule (3), the Speaker may order the security personnel to remove them.
(6) If a member is suspended under this rule, or if such suspension...
(7) If a member suspended under Sub-rule (3) or expelled under Rule 31 has a proposal that was previously announced to be presented, the Speaker may authorize any other member to present such a proposal.

33. Speaker's Special Authority to Maintain Order: (1) Notwithstanding anything contained elsewhere in this chapter, if any member disrupts or attempts to disrupt peace, order, and discipline in the meeting hall, or engages in or attempts to engage in destructive acts or use force that damages the prestige of the House, or causes physical damage, the Speaker may immediately order the security personnel to expel such a member from the meeting hall. The Speaker may suspend such an expelled member for a maximum of fifteen days and order them to compensate for the physical damage.
(2) A member suspended under Sub-rule (1) shall not be allowed to attend the meetings of the House or any committee during that period.
(3) If a member is suspended under this rule, or if such suspension... the Secretary shall inform the concerned committee and parliamentary party.
(4) Notwithstanding anything contained elsewhere in this regulation, if the Speaker becomes aware after the meeting has concluded that a member has behaved objectionably or destructively, or caused physical damage to the meeting hall, the Speaker may suspend such a member for a maximum of fifteen days and order them to compensate for the physical damage. However, before issuing such an order, the Speaker shall give the concerned member a reasonable opportunity to present their defense.

34. Withdrawal of Action: Notwithstanding anything contained elsewhere in this chapter, if an expelled or suspended member provides a satisfactory explanation or apologizes for their mistake, the Speaker may forgive such a member and withdraw the action.

35. Authority to Adjourn Meeting: If there is or is likely to be disorder in the meeting hall, or if it appears that the meeting cannot be conducted regularly, the Speaker may adjourn the meeting for a specified period on that day or for any time determined by the Speaker. No member shall raise questions about such an adjournment ordered by the Speaker.

Chapter 7
Address by the President and Policy and Program

36. Address by the President: (1) The President may address a joint meeting of both houses of the Federal Parliament or the House of Representatives as per Article 94 of the Constitution.
(2) The notice of the address as per Sub-rule (1) shall be given to the members by the Secretary General or, in his absence, by the Secretary.

37. Motion of Thanks: (1) After the President's address as per Rule 36, the Prime Minister shall table a copy of the address in the meeting.
(2) The Speaker shall present a motion of thanks for the address given by the President, and the motion shall be presented for decision.
(3) The Speaker shall present the motion of thanks passed by the House to the President.

38. Procedure Regarding Policy and Program: (1) After the motion of thanks for the President's address is passed, the Speaker shall, in consultation with the Prime Minister or any minister designated by him, determine the date and time for discussing the policy and program of the Government of Nepal mentioned in the President's address, and discussions shall be held on the matters mentioned in the address based on proportional representation of political parties.
(2) Amendments may be presented in the form deemed appropriate by the Speaker on the policy and program of the Government of Nepal mentioned in the President's address.
(3) The Prime Minister or the minister designated by him shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion as per Sub-rule (1) at the end of the discussion.
(4) After the Prime Minister or the minister designated by him has responded as per Sub-rule (3), the discussion shall be deemed concluded, and after decisions are made on the amendments presented, the Speaker shall present the policy and program to the House for decision.

39. Other Business That May Be Conducted: (1) On the day designated for discussion on the address as per Rule 38, the following business may also be conducted:-
(a) Completion of formal business before the commencement of the discussion,
(b) Presentation of a supplementary estimate as per Article 121 of the Constitution,
(c) Presentation of a motion seeking permission to present a bill or presentation of a bill.
(2) If a motion to adjourn the discussion is accepted, the following business may be conducted on the day designated for discussion on the address:-
(a) Presentation of a motion to pass a bill,
(b) Presentation of matters whose period for presentation in the meeting is specified by law,
(c) Discussion on urgent matters presented by a minister.

Chapter 8
Question Hour

40. Questions May Be Asked: (1) Any matter of public importance within the scope of work and responsibility of the Government of Nepal may be asked as a question in the meeting.
(2) Questions as per Sub-rule (1) may be registered after the commencement of the session of the House.

41. Question Answer Time: (1) Unless the Speaker orders otherwise, one hour of the first meeting of each day shall be allocated for question hour.
(2) After the question hour as per Sub-rule (1) concludes, other business as per the agenda shall commence.

42. Notice of Question: (1) A member wishing to ask a question shall provide notice to the Secretary, including the following:-
(a) The question to be asked,
(b) The minister concerned with the question,
(c) The member concerned in the case of Rule 47.
(2) If more than one member signs the notice, the first signatory shall be deemed to have given the notice as per Sub-rule (1). However, if the first signatory is absent, the subsequent member in order shall be considered.
(3) If more than one member gives notice of a question on the same subject, only the first registered question shall be considered. However, the names of all questioners shall be read out by the Speaker in the House.

43. Types of Questions: (1) Questions shall be of two types: oral and written.
(2) Answers that require reference to any record, data, etc., shall be given in writing.

44. List of Questions: (1) After receiving answers from the ministries, a separate list of questions for oral and written answers shall be prepared each day, and such a list shall include all questions not rejected. However, no more than twenty oral questions and fifty written questions shall be included in the list per day. No more than two oral questions from a single member shall be included in the list.
(2) The order of written questions shall be determined based on the time of receipt and subject matter.
(3) The order of oral questions shall be determined by lot.

45. Unanswered Questions to be Tabled: (1) If, due to lack of time, an oral question listed for a particular day cannot be answered, the concerned minister shall table a copy of the answer. However, if the minister requests the Speaker that answering an unanswered question is necessary in the public interest, the Speaker may allow the answer to be given after the time allocated for questions has ended.
(2) A copy of the tabled answer as per Sub-rule (1) shall be made available to the members.
(3) All oral and written questions that could not be answered during the current session and for which answers have been received from the ministries shall be tabled at the end of the session.

46. Answering Questions: (1) The Secretariat shall arrange for registered oral and written questions to be sent to the concerned ministries within five days.
(2) The concerned ministries shall provide answers to oral and written questions as per Sub-rule (1) to the Secretariat within a maximum of five days from the date of receipt of the questions. The Speaker shall manage the presentation of answers from the ministries to the House.
(3) A copy of written questions and answers shall be made available to each member.
(4) Other matters related to question hour shall be conducted as determined by the Speaker in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.

47. Questions to be Asked to the Proposing Member of a Motion or Bill: (1) Questions related to a motion or bill may be asked to any member other than a minister who is the proposer of the motion or bill.
(2) The Speaker may apply the procedures determined by the Speaker as needed regarding the asking and answering of questions as per Sub-rule (1).

48. Conditions for Questions: The following types of questions shall not be acceptable:-
(a) Vague,
(b) Too long, factually incorrect, meaningless, or trivial,
(c) Referring to any person or statement, unless essential for clarification, provided that the questioner shall be responsible for the accuracy of any name or statement included in the question.
(d) Suggesting an answer or promoting a particular viewpoint,
(e) Being a policy matter that cannot be answered or provided as information,
(f) Containing false statements, mere arguments, baseless assumptions, accusations, insinuations, sarcasm, or objectionable and unparliamentary words,
(g) Seeking solutions to hypothetical or speculative problems,
(h) Relating to a matter under consideration by a committee or a report already submitted to the House by a committee,
(i) Relating to a matter that can be easily ascertained through other means.

49. Matters on Which Questions Cannot Be Asked: (1) Questions shall not be asked on the following matters:-
(a) Matters prohibited from publication by the Constitution or any prevailing law,
(b) Matters that are inherently confidential or any decision or action of the Council of Ministers that would be contrary to national interest if published,
(c) Matters criticizing the House or any of its committees or decisions,
(d) Matters that have been clarified through discussion during the current session,
(e) Matters referring to any person or organization, etc., for propaganda purposes, directly or indirectly,
(f) Matters under consideration by a judicial or quasi-judicial body or authority established under prevailing law, or by a commission or committee formed to investigate, advise, or submit a report under prevailing law,
(g) Matters related to the interpretation of law,
(h) Matters concerning whether a statement made by a non-governmental person is true or false,
(i) Matters concerning the conduct of any person in their capacity other than their official or public capacity, or their personal conduct,
(j) Matters that have been rejected once or for which an answer has been refused under this regulation.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in clause (f) of Sub-rule (1), if the Speaker deems that it will not adversely affect the work of the commission or committee mentioned in that clause, questions may be allowed to be asked, limited to the procedure and progress of the investigation.

50. Decision on Acceptability of Questions: (1) The Speaker shall decide whether a question is acceptable or not. If the right to ask a question is misused, or if it is contrary to the Constitution or this regulation, or if it is likely to damage the prestige of the House or obstruct the proceedings of the meeting, the Speaker may reject such a question in whole or in part.
(2) If the Speaker deems it appropriate, they may make necessary amendments to the question without altering its intent, in accordance with the rules.
(3) Before making amendments as per Sub-rule (2), the Speaker may consult with the member asking the question.
(4) The Speaker's decision on whether a question is acceptable or not, and whether amendments can be made, shall be final.

51. Supplementary Questions May Be Asked by the Questioning Member: (1) The questioning member may ask supplementary questions.
(2) Supplementary questions must be clearly related to the original question.
(3) The conditions regarding questions under this regulation shall also apply to supplementary questions.

52. Withdrawal or Postponement of Questions: (1) A member may withdraw their question at any time before it is included in the agenda.
(2) A member may postpone their question to another day by giving two days' notice to the Secretary, and such postponed questions shall be included in the agenda of another day designated for that purpose.

53. Procedure for Question Hour: (1) After the question hour begins, the Speaker shall call the names of the questioners according to the agenda.
(2) The member whose name is called as per Sub-rule (1) or their authorized representative shall stand up and state the number of their question from the question list if they intend to ask a question.
(3) Questions not asked due to not being called shall be automatically removed from the question list.

54. Refusal to Answer: An answer to a question may be refused if it is deemed contrary to national or public interest.

55. Change of Question: The Speaker may change an oral question to a written question or a written question to an oral question if deemed appropriate.

Chapter 9
Direct Questioning of the Prime Minister

56. Direct Questions May Be Asked: (1) The Speaker shall designate the first hour of a meeting on one day of each month for asking questions directly related to important matters concerning the Prime Minister or their portfolio. If the meeting cannot be held on that day for any reason, the first hour of the immediately following meeting shall be designated.
(2) During the time as per Sub-rule (1), the Speaker shall call the names of the questioners.
(3) The member whose name is called as per Sub-rule (2) shall stand up and ask the Prime Minister a question.
(4) The Prime Minister shall answer the question as per Sub-rule (3) immediately.
(5) In asking questions under this rule, a maximum of fifteen members shall be allowed to ask questions per day, and after one member's question is answered, the name of the next questioner shall be called.

57. Direct Broadcasting May Be Done: The direct broadcasting of the direct question-answer program under this chapter may be done through government and other mass media.

58. Information to Be Provided: Members wishing to ask questions under this chapter shall provide written information about their questions to the Speaker three days before the meeting commences.

59. Conditions for Questions Applicable: (1) The conditions for questions mentioned in Rule 48 shall also apply to questions under this chapter, and questions shall not be asked on matters prohibited as per Rule 49.
(2) The decision on whether a question is acceptable or not shall be made as per Rule 50.
(3) Other procedures related to question hour shall be as determined by the Speaker.

60. Discussion Shall Not Be Held: No discussion shall be held in the House regarding the answer given under this chapter.

Chapter 10
Procedure for Proposals

61. No Discussion Without a Proposal: Unless otherwise provided in the Constitution and this regulation, no matter shall be discussed in the meeting without a proposal.

62. Conditions for Proposals: The following types of proposals shall not be acceptable:-
(a) Contrary to the subject matter prohibited by Article 105 of the Constitution or any other provision of the Constitution,
(b) Including more than one subject,
(c) With an unclear or indefinite subject matter,
(d) Containing mere arguments, assumptions, sarcasm, irrelevance, insinuations, or any other speculative or objectionable content,
(e) Referring to the conduct of any person in their personal capacity rather than their public or government capacity,
(f) Criticizing a decision of the House of Representatives or any of its committees, or a matter under consideration by the House or a committee,
(g) Referring to a matter under consideration by a judicial or quasi-judicial body or authority established under prevailing law, or by a commission or committee formed to investigate, advise, or submit a report under prevailing law,
(h) Repeating discussions already held during the current session,
(i) Using indecent and unparliamentary language,
(j) Matters related to criminal incidents, sexual violence, and discrimination where confidentiality is not maintained as per law,
(k) Matters related to a decision of the Council of Ministers where sensitive issues such as national security, integrity, or diplomatic relations may be affected, as certified by the Council of Ministers.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in clause (g) of Sub-rule (1), if the Speaker deems that it will not adversely affect the work of the commission or committee mentioned in that clause, the Speaker may permit proposals to be presented, limited to the procedure and progress of the investigation.

63. Decision on Acceptability of Proposal: The Speaker shall decide whether a proposal is acceptable or not, and in doing so, the Speaker may reject any part or the entire proposal, stating the reasons.

64. Setting Date and Time for Discussion: (1) The Speaker may set the date and time for discussion on a proposal, keeping in mind the workload of the House of Representatives.
(2) Unless otherwise provided in this regulation, after the allocated time for discussion expires, the Speaker shall present all necessary proposals for decision on the main subject to the House without allowing further discussion.

65. Proposal Decided and Cannot Be Resubmitted: (1) A proposal for which permission to present has been granted but which has not been presented shall be automatically invalidated.
(2) Unless otherwise provided in this regulation, the same subject matter that has been discussed or decided upon during the current session of the House of Representatives cannot be presented again through any other proposal.
(3) A proposal registered during the current session that has not been discussed shall be automatically invalidated.
However, if the proposing member submits a request to the Speaker to keep the proposal valid on the grounds that it is timely and appropriate, it shall be decided by the Speaker.

66. No Discussion Before Scheduled Time: No proposal or amendment shall be brought for discussion on any matter for which a time has been set for discussion in the meeting or which has been included in the agenda of the meeting before the scheduled time.

67. Proposal May Be Withdrawn: (1) A proposal registered in the Secretariat may be withdrawn by the proposer through a written request with the permission of the Speaker.
However, a proposal that has already been presented in the meeting cannot be withdrawn without the permission of the meeting, and if any amendment has been presented on the proposal, the proposal cannot be withdrawn until the amendment is resolved.
(2) If more than one member submits proposals on substantially the same subject, discussion shall be held only on the first proposal as per mutual agreement or registration order.

68. Notice of Proposal: A member wishing to present a proposal shall give written notice to the Secretary.

69. Procedure to Be Adopted Regarding Proposal: In relation to proposals, the procedures mentioned in this chapter shall be followed, and matters not mentioned in this chapter shall be governed by the provisions of Chapter 13.

70. Proposal May Be Presented Without Notice: (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this chapter, any of the following proposals may be presented without notice with the permission of the Speaker, and such proposals shall not raise any controversial issues:-
(a) Expressing thanks,
(b) Adjourning the meeting,
(c) Withdrawing proposals and amendments,
(d) Offering congratulations,
(e) Expressing condolences,
(f) Adjourning discussion,
(g) Extending the duration of discussion,
(h) Extending the duration of the meeting,
(i) Special proposal.
(2) After obtaining the Speaker's permission as per Sub-rule (1) and presenting the proposal in the meeting, the Speaker shall present the proposal to the House for decision.

71. Urgent Public Interest Proposal: (1) If a member wishes to discuss a matter of urgent public interest, they shall submit a proposal to the Secretary at least two hours before the meeting commences, and such a proposal must be supported by at least two members.
(2) Along with the notice of the proposal as per Sub-rule (1), the reason for discussing the proposal shall be clearly stated.
(3) If the Speaker deems the subject matter of the proposal as per Sub-rule (1) to be of urgent public interest, they shall grant approval for the proposal. After approval, the date and time for discussion shall be set in consultation with the concerned minister.
(4) The Speaker shall allocate a maximum of two hours for the discussion.
(5) After the proposing member gives a brief statement, the Speaker shall allocate time to the members participating in the discussion.
(6) The concerned minister shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion.

72. Motion for Attention: (1) A member may submit a motion to draw the attention of the concerned minister to a matter of public importance without the Speaker's prior permission.
(2) The concerned minister may choose to respond immediately in the meeting to the motion for attention as per Sub-rule (1), or request time to respond on another day.
(3) After the concerned minister responds as per Sub-rule (2), there shall be no discussion on it. However, members whose names are on the list of attention-grabbing motions may seek clarification.
(4) A member may draw attention only once per meeting, and attention shall not be drawn more than twice in a single meeting.
(5) A member wishing to submit a motion for attention shall give notice one day in advance.
(6) A maximum of two attention-grabbing motions shall be included in the agenda for a day, based on the order of registration. Only two such motions shall be included in the agenda if approved by the Speaker.

73. No Other Proposals May Be Presented: Except for proposals under Rules 70, 71, and 72, no other proposals shall be presented under this chapter.

Chapter 19
Motion for Adjournment

74. Notice: (1) Subject to this regulation, any member may, with the permission of the Speaker, present a motion to adjourn the meeting to discuss a matter of extreme public importance.
However, (a) if the Speaker deems the proposal to be contrary to the rules or if the Speaker rejects it, the Speaker may inform the proposing member of the reasons for rejection. (b) The Speaker may seek further information from the concerned minister before approving or rejecting the proposal if they deem it necessary.
(2) Notice of such a motion shall be given to the Secretary two hours before the meeting begins on the day the motion is sought to be presented. A copy of the notice, along with the motion, shall be provided to the Speaker, the concerned minister, and the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs.
(3) No more than one motion shall be presented as per Sub-rule (2).
(4) If more than one member signs the notice of the motion, the first signatory shall be deemed to have given the notice.

75. Conditions for Adjournment Motion: An adjournment motion may be presented subject to the following conditions:-
(a) The subject of the motion must be a recent event and within the scope of responsibility of the Government of Nepal.
(b) The conditions mentioned in Rule 62 must be fulfilled.

76. Permission of the House to Present Adjournment Motion: (1) After permission is granted to present a motion as per Rule 74, the Speaker shall call the name of the proposing member at the commencement of the meeting, and thereafter, the member wishing to present the adjournment motion shall seek permission from the House to present the motion.
(2) If any member opposes the motion, the Speaker shall order the members in favor of the motion to stand up. If twenty-five or more members stand up, the motion shall be deemed to have received permission from the House to be presented.

77. Setting Time for Discussion on Motion: The Speaker may set a time on the same day for discussion on the adjournment motion.

78. Conclusion and Decision of Discussion: (1) If the discussion on the motion is concluded, the Speaker shall declare it concluded and present the motion to the House for decision.
(2) If the adjournment motion presented for decision as per Sub-rule (1) is rejected, it shall not hinder the conduct of the remaining business on the agenda for that day.

Chapter 12
Substitute Motions

79. Substitute Motions May Be Presented: (1) Subject to this regulation, a member may present a substitute motion on any of the following matters of public importance:-
(a) To express public opinion or recommendations,
(b) To express support or opposition to any action or policy of the Government of Nepal or to draw attention for reconsideration,
(c) To give any message or directive.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in Sub-rule (1), the Speaker may permit the presentation of a substitute motion on any other matter deemed appropriate and necessary by him.

80. Notice: (1) The proponent of a substitute motion shall submit notice of the motion to the Secretary. Such a motion may be discussed in any meeting seven days after its registration.
(2) The Secretariat shall promptly provide the registered motion as per Sub-rule (1) to the concerned minister.
(3) A member shall not give notice of more than three substitute motions in the same session without the Speaker's permission.

81. Conditions for Substitute Motions: In addition to the provisions of this chapter, the conditions mentioned in Rule 62 shall also apply to substitute motions.

82. Decision on Acceptability: (1) The Speaker shall decide whether a substitute motion is acceptable or not, and shall reject any substitute motion or part thereof if it is contrary to the Constitution or this regulation.
(2) The Speaker shall distribute the substitute motion to the members for presentation within two days of its approval.

83. Substitute Motions to Be Presented: More than one substitute motion shall not be included in the agenda for a single meeting.

84. Method of Presenting Substitute Motion: (1) After the Speaker calls the name, the proponent of the substitute motion may present their motion in the meeting by reading out the details of the motion and giving a statement.
However, with the Speaker's permission, a member may authorize another member to present their substitute motion.
(2) If, after the Speaker calls the name as per Sub-rule (1), the member expresses unwillingness to present the motion, it shall be deemed that the motion has been withdrawn.

85. Time Limit for Discussion: The Speaker shall determine the time limit for the proponent member and other members wishing to speak.
At the end of the discussion, the concerned minister shall respond.

86. Conditions for Amendment: (1) Amendments may be presented to substitute motions with the Speaker's permission.
(2) The conditions mentioned in Rule 90 shall also apply to amendments of substitute motions.
(3) If more than one member gives notice of an amendment, the Speaker shall approve the amendment deemed appropriate.

87. Decision on Motion: After the discussion on the substitute motion is concluded, the House shall first make a decision on any amendments presented, and then on the substitute motion itself.

88. Approved Substitute Motions to Be Sent: A copy of each approved substitute motion shall be sent to the Council of Ministers and the concerned ministry for action. The concerned ministry shall regularly provide progress reports on the implementation of the approved substitute motion to the relevant committee and the Secretariat every three months.

89. Monitoring and Evaluation of Implemented Substitute Motions: (1) The concerned committee shall have the responsibility of monitoring the implementation of substitute motions passed by the House of Representatives within its area of jurisdiction.
(2) The committee shall mention the actual status of the monitoring and evaluation in its annual report.

Chapter 13
Amendment of Proposals

90. Conditions for Amendment: The following conditions must be met when presenting an amendment proposal in relation to any proposal:-
(a) It shall not be contrary to the principle of the original proposal,
(b) It shall be relevant to the subject matter of the original proposal,
(c) It shall not contradict a decision already made by the House,
(d) It shall have a clear meaning.

91. Notice of Amendment: (1) A member wishing to present an amendment shall give notice to the Secretary twenty-four hours before the day the proposal is to be discussed.
(2) Upon receiving notice of an amendment, a copy shall be made available to each member.
(3) An amendment notice cannot be submitted on a proposal that is not to be presented for decision in the House.

92. Approval of Amendment: (1) The Speaker shall have the authority to approve or reject an amendment proposal presented on a proposal.
(2) For the purpose of approval, the Speaker may ask the concerned member to clarify the purpose of the amendment, whether approved or rejected.

93. Presentation of Amendment: The Speaker shall present one of the amendments received on a proposal that is deemed appropriate to the House for decision, after reading it out to the House. The approved amendment or the proposal with the amendment or the original proposal shall then be presented for decision.

Chapter 14
Ordinance

94. Presentation of Ordinance: (1) An ordinance issued under Article 194 of the Constitution shall be presented by the concerned minister in the first meeting of the House convened after its issuance.
(2) When presenting an ordinance as per Sub-rule (1), a description of the circumstances and reasons necessitating its issuance shall be attached.
(3) The ordinance issued as per Sub-rule (1) shall be made available to the Secretariat as soon as possible.

95. Notice to Reject Ordinance: (1) A member wishing to present a motion to reject an ordinance shall submit such notice to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary within two days of the presentation of the ordinance.
(2) The notice shall include the reasons for rejecting the ordinance.
(3) If more than one notice is received as per Sub-rule (1), only one proposal as per mutual agreement or registration order shall be considered.

96. Method of Presenting Motion to Reject Ordinance: (1) After the Speaker calls the name, the member giving notice of the motion to reject the ordinance may present their motion by reading out the details of the motion and giving a statement.
(2) With the Speaker's permission, the member giving notice of the motion may authorize another member to present their motion.
(3) If, after the Speaker calls the name as per Sub-rule (1), the member expresses unwillingness to present the motion, it shall be deemed that the motion has been withdrawn.

97. Discussion and Decision on Motion to Reject Ordinance: (1) Other members who wish to present a motion to reject the ordinance as per this chapter may also participate in the discussion.
(2) At the end of the discussion as per Sub-rule (1), the concerned minister shall respond to the questions raised.
(3) After the minister responds as per Sub-rule (2), the Speaker shall present the motion to reject the ordinance to the House for decision.

98. Decision on Ordinance: (1) If no motion to reject the ordinance is received as per this chapter, or if the motion to reject the ordinance is rejected by the House, the Speaker shall allow the concerned minister to present a motion to approve the ordinance.
(2) The motion to approve the ordinance as per Sub-rule (1) shall be presented to the House for decision.

99. Bill to Replace Approved Ordinance: (1) A bill presented with the objective of replacing the provisions of an approved ordinance, either as is or with amendments, shall be accompanied by a description of the circumstances and reasons necessitating its immediate implementation.
(2) The procedure as per Chapter 15 shall be followed regarding a bill presented as per Sub-rule (1).
(3) A bill to replace an ordinance as per Sub-rules (1) and (2) must be passed within the period specified by Article 114 of the Constitution and authenticated by the President.

Chapter 15
Procedure for Bills

100. Notice for Permission to Present Bill: (1) Subject to the Constitution and this regulation, any member may present a bill. A member wishing to present a bill shall submit a notice along with the conceptual framework, objective and reasons for the bill, explanatory notes, and a note on delegated legislation as per Rule 103 to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary.
(2) If a bill, upon becoming an Act, incurs financial expenditure, a financial memorandum detailing the potential minimum financial liability shall also be attached when presenting such a bill.

101. Dependent Bill May Be Presented: A dependent bill, either in whole or in part, on a bill under consideration by the House may be presented even before the main bill is passed.
However, the dependent bill shall not be considered until the main bill is passed.

102. Bills on the Same Subject Shall Not Be Listed: Unless the Speaker orders otherwise, any other bill with the same subject matter and objective that is presented before or after a bill is under consideration in the House shall not be listed in the agenda. The bill presented later shall be considered to have been presented in relation to the bill under consideration.

103. Note on Management of Delegated Legislation: If any bill contains a provision for the delegation of authority, a detailed note on the reason for delegating such authority, the nature and scope of the delegated authority, and its potential impact shall be mandatorily attached.

104. Distribution of Bill: After a bill is registered in the Secretariat, a copy of the bill shall be made available to each member within two days for government bills and four days for non-governmental bills. Such a bill shall also be posted on the website on the same day.

105. Notice of Opposition to Motion Seeking Permission to Present Bill: A member wishing to oppose a motion seeking permission to present a bill shall give notice to the Secretary at least two days before the day the motion is to be presented.

106. Motion Seeking Permission to Present Bill: (1) A motion seeking permission to present a bill may be presented in the House on any meeting day after nine days from the date of notice. However, for government bills, notice may be given seven days in advance.
(2) If anyone opposes the motion seeking permission to present a bill, the Speaker shall allow the opposing member and the proposing member to give brief statements, and thereafter, the Speaker shall present the motion to the House for decision without further discussion.
(3) If permission to present the bill is granted as per Sub-rule (2), the proposing member shall immediately present the bill to the House.
(4) Notwithstanding anything contained elsewhere in this regulation, in the case of a bill originating from the National Assembly, the proposing member may present a motion as per Rule 107.

107. General Discussion on Bill: (1) In the meeting following the presentation of a bill as per Rule 106, the proposing member may present one of the following motions:-
(a) That the bill be considered,
(b) That the bill be circulated for public feedback.
(2) The proposing member shall inform the Speaker one day in advance which motion they intend to present as per Sub-rule (1)(a) or (b).
(3) If the proposing member presents a motion as per Sub-rule (1)(a) or (b), any other member may present another motion as per the same sub-rule. Such a motion shall be presented to the House for decision immediately.
(4) During the discussion on the motion as per Sub-rule (1)(a), discussion shall be held only on the theoretical aspect. Discussion on the clauses of the bill shall not be held, except when necessary to clarify the intent of the bill, and no amendments shall be presented on the bill.

108. Procedure for Obtaining Public Feedback: (1) If a motion to circulate a bill for public feedback is approved, the Secretary shall publish the bill in the Nepal Gazette and also through other appropriate means, specifying the period for collecting opinions. The collected opinions shall be compiled and provided to the proposing member through the Speaker.
(2) Other procedures for obtaining public feedback on a bill shall be as determined by the Speaker.

109. Procedure After Public Feedback is Obtained: After the motion as per Sub-rule (1) of Rule 108 is approved, the proposing member may present a motion to the House for consideration of the bill along with the collected public opinions.

110. Procedure After General Discussion is Concluded: (1) After the general discussion on a bill is concluded, the proposing member may present one of the following motions:-
(a) That the bill be discussed clause by clause in the House,
(b) That the bill be sent to the concerned committee for clause-by-clause discussion.
(2) If the proposing member presents a motion as per Sub-rule (1)(a) or (b), any other member may present another motion as per the same sub-rule. Such a motion shall be presented to the House for decision immediately.

111. Authorized Member May Present Bill: (1) If the proposing member is unable to be present, and if the Speaker is satisfied, another member authorized by the proposing member may be permitted to present the bill.
(2) In the case of a non-governmental bill originating from the National Assembly and passed, the Speaker may permit a member authorized by the proposing member to present a motion seeking consideration of the bill.

112. Notice of Amendment to Bill: Any member wishing to present an amendment to a bill shall give notice to the Secretary within seventy-two hours after the conclusion of the general discussion on the bill.

113. Conditions for Amendment: (1) Amendments to a bill may be presented subject to the following conditions:-
(a) It shall be relevant to the subject matter of the bill,
(b) It shall be in line with the principle enshrined in the bill,
(c) It shall be clear and meaningful,
(d) It shall be relevant to the specific clause or wording sought to be amended,
(e) It shall be in line with principles or decisions previously approved by the House,
(f) It shall clearly state the specific provision or wording of the bill sought to be amended and the proposed provision or wording.
(2) The Speaker shall have the authority to approve or reject an amendment under this rule, or to call the concerned member and ask them to improve it, or to consolidate amendments of similar intent and approve them.

114. Details of Amendments: (1) A consolidated list of amendments approved by the Speaker shall be prepared, arranged clause-wise as far as possible.
(2) The consolidated list of approved amendments shall be made available to the members and shall also be posted on the website on the same day.

115. Discussion on Amendments: (1) Discussion on amendments shall generally be held clause-wise in order, and the member whose name is called by the Speaker or their authorized representative may present the amendment.
(2) The Speaker shall present each clause of the bill, whether unchanged or with amendments accepted, to the House for decision in order, stating "Let this clause be part of the bill".
However, for the sake of time efficiency, discussion on multiple clauses or interdependent amendments under a clause may be allowed to be held together, and parts or clauses of the bill may be deferred for discussion later or brought forward for discussion later.

Clarification: For the purposes of this rule and Rules 116, 117, 118, and 119, "Speaker" shall mean the Chairperson of the committee, and "Meeting" shall mean the meeting of the concerned committee.

116. Amendment May Be Withdrawn: (1) The proposing member of an amendment may withdraw the amendment with the permission of the House.
However, if an amendment has been presented to an amendment, the original amendment cannot be withdrawn until the amendment to the amendment is resolved.
(2) If the proposing member of an amendment is unable to attend the meeting, they may authorize another member to present or withdraw the amendment with the Speaker's permission.

117. Discussion on Schedules: Unless the Speaker decides otherwise, discussion on schedules and amendments thereto shall be held immediately after the discussion on the corresponding clauses, and the proposals for decision by the House shall also be presented in the same order.

118. Clauses May Be Presented Collectively for Decision: The Speaker may, if deemed appropriate, present clauses, schedules, amended clauses, or schedules of the bill collectively for decision by the House.
However, any member may request that a clause or schedule, or an amended clause or schedule, be presented separately for decision, and such a proposal shall be presented separately for decision.

119. Presentation of Preamble and Title: Only after all other clauses and schedules of the bill have been decided upon, the Speaker shall, at the end, present the preamble and title-related clauses to the House for adoption as part of the bill, either in their original or amended form.

120. Directives May Be Given: When sending a bill under consideration to a committee, or while it is under consideration in a committee, the House may direct the concerned committee to expand or include or exclude specific provisions of the bill, provided that such directives are not contrary to the fundamental spirit of the bill.

121. Committee Report: After the committee's report is presented, it shall be distributed to the members. The Chairperson of the concerned committee, or any other member designated by the Chairperson in his absence, shall present the bill along with the report to the House.

122. Bill May Be Withdrawn: (1) A member wishing to present a motion seeking permission to withdraw a bill shall give notice one day in advance.
(2) If a motion seeking permission to withdraw a bill is presented while the bill is under consideration in the concerned committee, the concerned committee shall submit a report to the House stating the same.
(3) The proposing member may present a motion to withdraw the bill with the Speaker's permission.
(4) If anyone opposes the motion to withdraw the bill, the Speaker may allow the opposing member and the proposing member to give brief statements, and thereafter, without further discussion, shall present the motion to the House for decision.
(5) If a bill originating from the National Assembly is under consideration in the House of Representatives, permission shall be granted to the proposing member to present a motion seeking the recommendation of the House to obtain the consent of the National Assembly. If such a motion is approved and the National Assembly consents, the proposing member may present a motion to withdraw the bill.

123. Procedure After Committee Report is Presented: (1) After the committee's report is presented, it shall be distributed to the members.
(2) Unless the Speaker orders otherwise, in any meeting held twenty-four hours after the distribution of the report, the proposing member of the bill may present one of the following motions:-
(a) That the bill be considered along with the report,
(b) That the bill or specific clauses thereof be sent back to the concerned committee for reconsideration with directives.
(3) If a member presents a motion as per Sub-rule (2), any other member may present another motion as per the same sub-rule.
(4) If motions are presented as per Sub-rule (3), the Speaker shall give the proposing member and the other proposing member an opportunity to speak, and after both have spoken, and if the proposing member does not withdraw the proposal, the motion shall be presented to the House for decision without further discussion.
(5) If a decision is made to send the bill back to the committee, the bill shall be returned to the concerned committee for reconsideration and submission of a report.
(6) The committee shall promptly reconsider the bill returned for reconsideration as per Sub-rule (5) and present the bill along with its report to the House.
(7) After the report submitted by the committee as per Sub-rule (6) is distributed to the members, the proposing member may present a motion as per Sub-rule (2)(a) on the day and time specified by the Speaker.
(8) If the motion as per Sub-rule (2)(a) or Sub-rule (7) is approved, discussion shall be held only on the clauses of the bill and related schedules as per the committee's report.

124. Motion to Pass Bill: After the clause-by-clause discussion in the meeting is concluded, and after the discussion on the committee's report is concluded and amendments are presented to the House for decision one by one, the proposing member of the bill shall present a motion to pass the bill.

125. Bill to Be Removed from Record: (1) A bill shall be removed from the record in the following circumstances:-
(a) If the bill is rejected by the House:-
(1) Motion seeking permission to present the bill,
(2) Motion seeking consideration of the bill along with the committee's report, or
(3) Motion seeking to pass the bill or the bill with amendments.
(b) If the bill is withdrawn,
(c) If the proposing member of a non-governmental bill ceases to be a member of the House,
(d) If a non-governmental bill proposing member becomes a minister while the bill is still under consideration.
(2) No motion shall be presented in the meeting regarding a bill removed from the record as per Sub-rule (1).

126. Bill Cannot Be Resubmitted: A bill rejected by the House shall not be resubmitted during the same session.

127. Bill to Be Sent to National Assembly: (1) A bill passed by the House shall be sent to the National Assembly with a message.
(2) The Secretary shall certify the bill to be sent to the National Assembly as per Sub-rule (1), indicating the date it was passed by the House.
(3) The Speaker shall certify on the first page of a money bill that it is a money bill.

128. Message of Bill Passed by National Assembly: If a message is received after a bill passed by the House and sent to the National Assembly is passed by the National Assembly without amendment, the Secretary shall inform the House if the session is in progress, and if the session is not in progress, it shall be published in the notice paper of the House.

129. Money Bill Returned with Suggestions by National Assembly: (1) If a message is received after a money bill passed by the House is returned by the National Assembly with suggestions, the Secretary shall table it in the House as soon as possible, and a copy shall be distributed to the members.
(2) In a meeting held after the distribution of the copy of the message as per Sub-rule (1), the Finance Minister may, with the Speaker's permission, present a motion seeking consideration of the suggestions from the National Assembly.
(3) The Speaker may adopt any procedure deemed appropriate for discussion and decision on the suggestions from the National Assembly as per Sub-rule (2).
(4) Suggestions deemed appropriate by the House to be included shall be incorporated into the money bill, and the Speaker shall present it to the President for authentication. The National Assembly shall be informed accordingly.

130. Money Bill Returned Without Suggestions or Not Returned Within the Deadline by National Assembly: (1) If the National Assembly returns a money bill without suggestions, the Secretary shall table the message in the House.
(2) If the money bill is not returned by the National Assembly within one hundred days as per Sub-rule (1), the Speaker shall inform the House of this fact.
(3) The Speaker shall certify the money bill as per Sub-rule (1) or (2) and present it to the President for authentication as soon as possible.

131. Other Bills Returned with Amendments by National Assembly: (1) If a message is received after a bill passed by the House and sent to the National Assembly is returned by the National Assembly with amendments, the Secretary shall table the message in the House as soon as possible, and a copy of the message with amendments shall be distributed to the members.
(2) In a meeting held after the distribution of the message with amendments as per Sub-rule (1), the proposing member of the bill or their authorized representative may, with the Speaker's permission, present a motion seeking consideration of the amendments.
(3) The Speaker may adopt any procedure deemed appropriate for discussion and decision on the motion as per Sub-rule (2).
(4) If the House agrees with the amendments made by the National Assembly, the Speaker shall present the bill to the President for authentication as soon as possible.

132. Bill Rejected by National Assembly, Not Returned Within Deadline, or Amendments Not Acceptable: If the House decides by a majority of more than fifty percent of the total members currently present in the House to pass a bill that has been rejected by the National Assembly, not returned within the deadline, or whose amendments are not acceptable, the Speaker shall certify such a bill and present it to the President for authentication:-
(a) If the National Assembly does not pass or return with amendments a bill passed by the House and sent to it within two months,
(b) If the bill is rejected by the National Assembly and returned,
(c) If the amendments made by the National Assembly are not acceptable.

133. Bills Originating in National Assembly and Passed: (1) When a message is received after a bill originating in the National Assembly is passed by that House, the Secretary shall table it in the House, and a copy shall be distributed to the members.
(2) Two days after the distribution of the copy of the bill along with the message as per Sub-rule (1), the proposing member may present a motion seeking consideration of the bill.
However, the Speaker may permit such a motion to be presented in any meeting after the distribution of the copy of the bill, if deemed appropriate.
(3) During the discussion on the motion as per Sub-rule (2), discussion shall be held on the principle of the bill and matters related thereto. Discussion on clauses shall not be held, except when necessary to clarify the principle of the bill.
(4) The procedure following the approval of the motion as per Sub-rule (2) shall be the same as the procedure for bills originating in the House.
(5) If the motion "Let the bill be considered" is rejected, or if the bill is passed, or passed with amendments, the Secretary shall send a message to the National Assembly clarifying the details accordingly.

134. Speaker's Authority to Correct Minor Errors: The Speaker may, if deemed necessary, correct the numbering of clauses, make necessary consequential amendments, and correct minor typographical or accidental errors in a bill passed by the House.

135. Authentication of Bill: (1) The Speaker shall authenticate a bill passed by the House as per Article 113(1) of the Constitution.
(2) When authenticating as per Sub-rule (1), the Speaker shall also certify that it is a money bill in the case of a money bill.

136. Presentation of Bill for Authentication: (1) The authenticated bill as per Rule 135 shall be presented by the Speaker to the President for authentication as per Article 113(2) of the Constitution.
(2) After receiving the notice of authentication by the President, the Speaker shall read out the notice in the meeting. If the session is not in progress, the Secretary shall publish such notice in the notice paper and make it public.
(3) The bill shall be publicly available through electronic media from the date of authentication until it is not authenticated.

137. Bill Returned by President with Message: (1) If a bill is returned by the President with a message for reconsideration as per Article 113(3) of the Constitution, the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall inform the House if the session is in progress, and if the session is not in progress, it shall be published in the notice paper of the House.
(2) After the information regarding the bill received as per Sub-rule (1) is provided to the House, the proposing member of the bill may present one of the following motions in a meeting held thereafter:-
(a) That the bill be reconsidered by the House,
(b) That the bill be sent to the concerned committee for reconsideration.
(3) The procedure related to the bill as per Sub-rule (1) shall be as per this chapter.

138. Laws Made Under Delegated Legislation to Be Made Available: (1) Laws made under authority delegated by the Parliament shall be made available to the concerned committee or the Secretariat of the House of Representatives within fifteen days from the date of their approval by the relevant body and publication in the Gazette.
(2) The concerned committee shall examine the laws made under delegated legislation made available as per Sub-rule (1) based on the matters mentioned in Rule 179, prioritizing them.
(3) If, during the examination as per Sub-rule (2), any provision is found to be beyond the scope of authority delegated by the Parliament, the committee may direct that such provisions not be implemented and that they be revised and submitted to the committee immediately.

139. Measurement of Law Implementation: (1) The committee may measure the effectiveness or implementation problems or areas for improvement and justification of laws that have been in effect for at least two years within its area of jurisdiction.
(2) The procedure for measuring law implementation shall be as determined by the Speaker.

140. Procedure for Constitutional Amendment Bill: (1) A constitutional amendment bill presented to the House as per Article 274 of the Constitution shall be published publicly for the information of the general public by the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary within thirty days of its presentation. However, a constitutional amendment bill originating from the National Assembly and presented with a message shall not be published as per this sub-rule.
(2) The bill published as per Sub-rule (1) may be presented in any meeting held seven days thereafter.
(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in Sub-rule (2), in the case of matters mentioned in Article 274(4) and Schedule-6 of the Constitution, the bill must be sent to the Provincial Assemblies for consent within the period specified in that sub-clause and schedule. However, a bill that has been presented in the National Assembly and has already obtained the consent of the Provincial Assemblies shall not be sent again for consent as per this sub-rule.
(4) If the House is informed that a majority of Provincial Assemblies have rejected such a bill within the period specified in Article 274(5) of the Constitution, the bill shall be invalidated, and no further action shall be taken on it.
(5) If information is not received within the period specified in Article 274(5) by the deadline specified in Sub-rule (6), the proposing member may present a motion seeking consideration of the bill in any meeting held thereafter. However, this sub-rule shall not hinder the proposing member from presenting a motion seeking consideration of a bill that does not require consent from the Provincial Assemblies as per Sub-rule (3), in any meeting after its presentation as per Sub-rule (2).
(6) After the motion as per Sub-rule (5) is approved, amendments may be presented limited to the specific clauses, sub-clauses, clauses, or explanations where amendments have been proposed in the bill.
(7) The Speaker may, with the permission of the House, form a special committee to discuss the constitutional amendment bill and related proposed amendments clause by clause and present the bill with the report to the House.
(8) The Speaker shall present each clause or sub-clause of the constitutional amendment bill, or any amendment proposed thereto, to the House for decision one by one.
(9) A bill to amend the Constitution shall be deemed passed if the motion to pass the bill is approved by at least two-thirds majority of the total members currently present in the House.
(10) The decision of the House on the motion to pass a bill to amend the Constitution shall be made by a division of votes with signatures of the members.
(11) If a constitutional amendment bill passed by the House and sent to the National Assembly is received with a message, and if the total votes in favor in both houses reach at least two-thirds of the total members currently present, the Speaker shall authenticate the bill and send it to the President for authentication.
(12) Other procedures for constitutional amendment bills shall be as provided in this chapter, subject to this rule.

Chapter 16
Procedure Regarding Estimates

141. Revenue and Expenditure Estimates: (1) The Finance Minister shall present the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure in a joint meeting of the Federal Parliament as per Article 119 of the Constitution.
(2) After the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure are presented as per Sub-rule (1), the Finance Minister shall table a copy of the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure in the meeting held thereafter.
(3) Discussion shall not be held on the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure on the day they are tabled.

142. Discussion on Annual Estimates of Revenue and Expenditure: (1) Before the Finance Minister presents the motion seeking approval for the budget, the Speaker shall, in consultation with the Prime Minister or any other minister designated by him, determine the date and time for general discussion on the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure.
(2) General discussion on the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure shall be held on the date and within the time allocated as per Sub-rule (1).
(3) The discussion as per Sub-rule (2) shall commence with a statement by the Finance Minister, and the Finance Minister shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion at the end of the discussion.

143. Supplementary Estimates: (1) The Finance Minister may present supplementary estimates in a meeting of the House of Representatives as per Article 121 of the Constitution.
(2) When presenting supplementary estimates as per Sub-rule (1), the Finance Minister shall clarify the objective, reasons, and scope thereof.
(3) Discussion shall not be held on supplementary estimates on the day they are presented as per Sub-rule (1).
(4) The Speaker shall, in consultation with the Prime Minister or any other minister designated by him, determine the date and time for general discussion on the supplementary estimates.
(5) General discussion on the supplementary estimates shall be held on the date and within the time allocated as per Sub-rule (4).
(6) The discussion as per Sub-rule (5) shall commence with a statement by the Finance Minister, and the Finance Minister shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion at the end.

144. Expenditure Estimates: (1) The expenditure proposed for each ministry shall generally be kept under separate headings. However, the Finance Minister may include expenditure proposed for two or more ministries and departments under a single heading, or include expenditure that cannot be easily classified under a particular ministry under a single heading.
(2) Each heading shall first list the total proposed expenditure, followed by a detailed breakdown of expenditure under sub-headings. Discussion may be held in the meeting on such details.
However, expenditure from the Contingency Fund shall not require the approval of the meeting.

Chapter 17
Procedure Related to Appropriation and Finance Bills

145. Procedure for Appropriation Bill: (1) Notwithstanding anything contained elsewhere in this regulation, the procedure specified in this chapter shall be followed regarding the Appropriation Bill.
(2) Before presenting the Appropriation Bill, the Finance Minister shall present a motion in the meeting seeking discussion on the principles and priorities for budget preparation.
(3) The discussion as per Sub-rule (2) shall be held in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee and as determined by the Speaker.
(4) The discussion as per Sub-rule (2) shall be concluded at least fifteen days before the presentation of the annual estimates of revenue and expenditure in the meeting.
(5) The report prepared incorporating the issues raised in the discussion as per Sub-rule (2) shall be promptly submitted to the Council of Ministers, the Ministry of Finance, and the National Planning Commission by the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary.
(6) The Ministry of Finance shall prepare the Appropriation Bill based on the report submitted as per Sub-rule (5).
(7) After the motion "Let the Appropriation Bill be considered" is presented, the Speaker shall, in consultation with the Prime Minister or any other minister designated by him, determine the date and time for discussion and decision on the various headings of the bill.
(8) Each heading specified in the Appropriation Bill shall be discussed individually or collectively.
(9) During the discussion as per Sub-rule (8), the concerned minister shall respond to questions raised on any heading at the end of the discussion.
(10) Before presenting any heading for decision, if there are multiple proposals for expenditure reduction of the same nature based on the party structure, discussion shall be held on only one such proposal, and a decision shall be made.
(11) If separate proposals related to the expenditure amount of the same heading are presented, discussion shall be held in order based on the annual estimates.
(12) At the end of the date and time allocated as per Sub-rule (7), all headings on which discussion remains shall be presented by the Speaker for decision without discussion.
(13) After the decision as per Sub-rule (12), the Finance Minister shall present a motion to pass the Appropriation Bill. If such a motion is approved, the Appropriation Bill shall be deemed passed.

146. Motion for Expenditure Reduction: (1) A motion may be presented to reduce the expenditure amount of any heading as follows:-
(a) "Let the expenditure amount of the heading be reduced to one rupee."
(b) "Let the expenditure amount mentioned in the heading be reduced."
(c) "Let one hundred rupees be deducted from the expenditure amount of the heading."
(2) A proposal presented to express disagreement with the policy within a heading, stating "Let the expenditure amount of the heading be reduced to one rupee", shall be considered a proposal to express disagreement with the policy. The reasons for disagreement with the policy shall be clearly stated in such a notice. Discussion shall be limited to the specific points mentioned in the proposal, and alternative policy suggestions may be made in the proposal.
(3) A proposal presented as "Let the expenditure amount mentioned in the heading be reduced" shall be considered a proposal to achieve economy. Discussion shall be limited only to how economy can be achieved in such a proposal, and it may propose to abolish a sub-heading or reduce the expenditure amount of such a sub-heading. The notice for presenting such a proposal must clearly state the subject matter for discussion and the specific points on how economy can be achieved.
(4) A proposal presented as "Let one hundred rupees be deducted from the expenditure amount of the heading" shall be considered a symbolic reduction proposal presented by a member to express dissatisfaction with a matter within the scope of responsibility of the Government of Nepal. Discussion shall be limited only to the specific matter of dissatisfaction mentioned.

147. Conditions for Expenditure Reduction Proposal: In addition to the conditions mentioned in Rule 62, the following conditions must be met for a proposal to reduce the expenditure amount of any heading:-
(a) It shall relate to only one heading,
(b) It shall clearly state a specific matter,
(c) It shall be a matter within the scope of concern of the Government of Nepal,
(d) It shall not relate to expenditure from the Contingency Fund,
(e) It shall not be about a subject for which a time for discussion has already been set in the meeting.

148. Notice of Expenditure Reduction Proposal: A member wishing to present a proposal to reduce the expenditure amount of any heading shall give notice after the general discussion on the Appropriation Bill is concluded. When giving notice, only one notice shall be given per ministry per political party or independent member in the House.

149. Decision on Expenditure Reduction Proposal: The Speaker shall decide whether an expenditure reduction proposal is acceptable for discussion. If the proposal is found to be contrary to this regulation, the Speaker may allow it to be accepted after making necessary amendments.

150. Prohibition of Amendment to Expenditure Reduction Proposal: No amendment shall be presented to a proposal to reduce the expenditure amount of any heading.

151. Advance Expenditure Bill: (1) The Finance Minister may present an advance expenditure bill in the meeting as per Article 122(1) of the Constitution.
(2) The Speaker may adopt any procedure deemed appropriate regarding the advance expenditure bill presented as per Sub-rule (1).
(3) The procedure for presenting and passing a bill as per Article 122(2) of the Constitution shall be the same as for an advance expenditure bill.

152. Finance Bill: (1) In the meeting immediately following the presentation of the annual estimates, the Finance Minister shall present the Finance Bill enacted to implement the financial policies of the Government of Nepal.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained elsewhere in this regulation, the procedure specified in this rule shall be followed regarding the Finance Bill.
(3) After a motion to consider the Finance Bill is presented and approved, the Speaker shall, in consultation with the Prime Minister or any other minister designated by him, determine the date and time for discussion on the bill.
(4) During the discussion on the Finance Bill, any member may discuss matters related to the administrative functions within the scope of responsibility of the Government of Nepal, local issues, or the monetary and economic policies of the Government of Nepal.
(5) Procedures other than those specified in this rule regarding the Finance Bill shall be as determined by the Speaker.

153. Supplementary Appropriation Bill: (1) After the discussion on supplementary estimates is concluded, the Finance Minister shall present a supplementary appropriation bill in the meeting.
(2) The procedure related to the supplementary appropriation bill shall be the same as the procedure for the appropriation bill.

154. Loan Expenditure Bill: (1) The Finance Minister may present a loan expenditure bill in the meeting as per Article 123 of the Constitution.
(2) The Speaker may adopt any procedure deemed appropriate regarding the bill presented as per Sub-rule (1).

Chapter 18
Vote of Confidence and No-Confidence Motion

155. Vote of Confidence: (1) The Prime Minister shall present a motion seeking a vote of confidence from the House of Representatives within thirty days of being appointed as Prime Minister as per Article 76(2), (3), or (5) of the Constitution, or if the party represented by the Prime Minister splits, or if a party participating in the government withdraws its support. The notice for presenting such a motion shall be submitted to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary.
(2) The Prime Minister may, as per Article 100(1) of the Constitution, present a motion seeking a vote of confidence if he deems it necessary to clarify that he has the confidence of the House of Representatives at any time.
(3) After receiving the notice as per Sub-rule (1) or (2), the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall submit the proposal to the Speaker, and the Speaker shall set the date and time for presenting the proposal in a meeting held after receiving such notice.
(4) On the date and time specified as per Sub-rule (3), the Prime Minister shall, with the Speaker's permission, give his statement regarding the matter and present the motion to the House.
(5) The Speaker shall set a time limit for the discussion on the proposal as per Sub-rule (4), and at the end of that period, the Prime Minister or a minister authorized by him, if the Prime Minister is unable to be present due to illness or other unavoidable circumstances, shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion.
(6) After the Prime Minister or the minister authorized by him responds as per Sub-rule (5) to the question of whether the House has confidence in the Prime Minister, the Speaker shall present the motion to the House for decision.
(7) The decision on the motion shall be made by a division of votes with signatures of the members.
(8) If the Prime Minister fails to obtain a vote of confidence as per this rule, the Speaker shall inform the President accordingly.

156. Motion of No-Confidence: (1) As per Article 100(4) of the Constitution, one-fourth of the total members of the House may submit a written notice to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary, stating that the Prime Minister does not have the confidence of the House.
(2) After receiving the notice as per Sub-rule (1), the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall immediately inform the Speaker.
(3) The Speaker shall set the date and time for discussion on the motion of no-confidence, to be held in a meeting seven days after the date of receipt of the notice as per Sub-rule (1), and shall publish it in the notice paper for the advance information of the members.
(4) On the date and time specified as per Sub-rule (3), the Speaker shall call the name of the first signatory among the proposing members or the member authorized by them, and that member shall present the motion. If they wish, they may give a statement before presenting the motion.
(5) The Speaker shall set a time limit for the discussion on the motion as per Sub-rule (4), and at the end of that period, the Prime Minister or a minister authorized by him, if the Prime Minister is unable to be present due to illness or other unavoidable circumstances, shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion.
(6) After the Prime Minister or the minister authorized by him responds as per Sub-rule (5), the proposing member may, with the permission of the House, withdraw the motion.
(7) If the proposing member does not withdraw the motion as per Sub-rule (6), the Speaker shall present the motion to the House for decision.
(8) The decision on the motion of no-confidence shall be made by a division of votes with signatures of the members.
(9) If the motion as per this rule is passed by the total majority of the members currently present in the House, the Speaker shall inform the President accordingly.

Chapter 19
Motion of Impeachment

157. Motion of Impeachment Against President or Vice-President: (1) As per Article 101(1) of the Constitution, a motion of impeachment against the President or Vice-President shall be submitted to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary, stating the grounds and reasons for the impeachment.
(2) If the motion is submitted when the session of the House of Representatives is not in progress, the member submitting the motion shall request the convening of a session of the House of Representatives within a maximum of five days from the date of submission of the motion, as per Article 93(3) of the Constitution.
(3) After receiving the motion as per Sub-rule (1), the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall submit it to the Speaker. The date and time for discussion shall be set within seven days of the date of submission if the session is in progress, and in the first meeting after the session is convened if the session is not in progress.
(4) On the date and time specified as per Sub-rule (3), the Speaker shall call the name of the proposing member, and the member shall present the motion of impeachment to the House. If they wish, they may give a statement before presenting the motion.
(5) After the motion is presented as per Sub-rule (4), the Speaker shall set a time limit for the discussion on the motion. After the discussion concludes, the proposing member may, with the permission of the House, withdraw the motion.
(6) If the motion is not withdrawn as per Sub-rule (5), the Speaker may propose the formation of an eleven-member impeachment investigation committee in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.
(7) If the proposal to form an investigation committee as per Sub-rule (6) is approved, the Speaker shall send the impeachment motion along with the necessary documents to the committee within three days, and shall inform the President or Vice-President and the concerned bodies.
(8) The investigation committee shall have the authority to collect evidence regarding the impeachment, investigate whether the President or Vice-President has committed serious violations of the Constitution and federal laws, and record statements from the President or Vice-President.
(9) The members of the committee may elect a chairperson from among themselves.

158. Term of Investigation Committee: The term of the investigation committee shall be a maximum of three months from the date of commencement of its work.

159. Procedure of Committee: (1) The impeachment investigation committee shall commence its proceedings as soon as possible after receiving the impeachment motion and related documents.
(2) The chairperson of the investigation committee may manage the committee's proceedings under the direction of the Speaker.
(3) The investigation committee may seek assistance from the Secretariat and other officials as needed.

160. Report of Investigation Committee: (1) The investigation committee shall investigate each allegation, consider the grounds and reasons for impeachment, and present a report with recommendations to the House, indicating whether impeachment is justified or not, after giving the President or Vice-President a reasonable opportunity to present their defense.
(2) After the report is presented to the House as per Sub-rule (1), a copy shall be distributed to all members.

161. Consideration of Investigation Committee Report: (1) In the meeting held forty-eight hours after the report is presented as per Rule 160, the chairperson of the investigation committee, or any member designated by the Speaker in his absence, shall present a motion seeking consideration of the committee's report in the House.
(2) After the motion as per Sub-rule (1) is presented, the report shall be discussed in the meeting.
(3) During the discussion as per Sub-rule (2), any member may present a motion to send the report back to the committee for further investigation on any specific matter.
(4) If the motion to send the report back to the investigation committee as per Sub-rule (3) is approved, the report shall be sent back to the committee, and the committee shall investigate again within seven days and submit its report to the meeting.

162. Motion to Approve Investigation Committee Report: (1) After the discussion on the report as per Rule 161(2) is concluded, or after the report is received back in the meeting as per Rule 161(4), the chairperson of the committee, or any other member designated by the Speaker in his absence, may present a motion seeking approval of the recommendations mentioned in the report.
(2) After the motion as per Sub-rule (1) is presented, any member may present amendments to the report, if the Speaker deems appropriate.
(3) After a brief discussion on the motion presented as per Sub-rule (1) or the amendments presented as per Sub-rule (2), the Speaker shall present all amendments for decision to the House, and thereafter, the recommendations mentioned in the report shall be presented to the House for decision.
(4) The decision on the motion of impeachment shall be made by a division of votes with signatures of the members.
(5) If the motion of impeachment against the President or Vice-President as per this chapter is passed by a two-thirds majority of the total members currently present in the House, it shall be sent to the National Assembly with a message.
(6) Proceedings on a motion of impeachment as per Rule 1547 shall be concluded by the House of Representatives within a maximum of five months.
(7) If a motion as per Rule 1547 is submitted when the term of the House of Representatives is less than five months, the Speaker may decide to conclude the proceedings within the period determined by the Business Advisory Committee using a expedited procedure.
(8) If the proceedings cannot be concluded even through the expedited procedure adopted as per Sub-rule (7), or if the motion of impeachment remains on record, it shall be considered as a record for the new House of Representatives. The newly elected House of Representatives shall accept such a record within five months of its formation and make a necessary decision within the next five months.

163. Deemed Vacated Office: (1) If a motion of impeachment against the President or Vice-President as per Article 101(1) of the Constitution is passed by at least a two-thirds majority of the total members of both houses of the Federal Parliament, the concerned President or Vice-President shall be deemed to have automatically vacated their office.
(2) The Secretary General shall inform the Council of Ministers, the concerned ministry, and the Government of Nepal about the automatic vacation of office as per Sub-rule (1).
(3) If the President or Vice-President who has been impeached and removed from office has committed any offense while in office, such an offense shall not be hindered from being prosecuted under federal law as per Article 101(8) of the Constitution.
(4) A person impeached and removed from office shall not be entitled to any benefits from such office as per Article 101(9) of the Constitution and shall not be appointed or nominated to any public office in the future.

164. Motion of Impeachment Against Chief or Official of Constitutional Body: (1) As per Article 101(2) of the Constitution, a motion of impeachment against a chief or official of a constitutional body shall be submitted to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary, stating the grounds and reasons for the impeachment.

Explanation: For the purpose of this rule, "Official" shall mean the Chief Justice or a Justice of the Supreme Court, a member of the Judicial Council, a chief or official of a constitutional body, or a judge of the Supreme Court.
(2) If the motion is submitted when the session of the House of Representatives is not in progress, the member submitting the motion shall request the convening of a session of the House of Representatives within a maximum of five days from the date of submission of the motion, as per Article 93(3) of the Constitution.
(3) After receiving the motion as per Sub-rule (1), the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall submit it to the Speaker. The date and time for discussion shall be set within fifteen days of the date of submission if the session is in progress, and in the first meeting after the session is convened if the session is not in progress.
(4) On the date and time specified as per Sub-rule (3), the Speaker shall call the name of the proposing member, and the member shall present the motion of impeachment to the House. If they wish, they may give a statement before presenting the motion.
(5) After the motion is presented as per Sub-rule (4), the Speaker shall set a time limit for the discussion on the motion. After the discussion concludes, the Speaker shall send the motion to a three-member impeachment investigation committee within three days.
(6) If the grounds and reasons for impeachment are found to be sufficient based on the information, notice, or complaint submitted by three members as per Article 101(2) of the Constitution, the impeachment investigation committee may submit a recommendation for impeachment proceedings.
(7) If, during the preliminary investigation by the committee as per Sub-rule (6), reasonable and sufficient grounds for impeachment are found against the concerned individual, the committee may submit a recommendation for impeachment. If such a recommendation is presented to the House for decision, the impeachment proceedings shall commence if supported by one-fourth of the total members currently present in the House of Representatives.
(8) After impeachment proceedings commence as per Sub-rules (1) and (7), the Chief Justice or a Justice of the Supreme Court, a member of the Judicial Council, or a chief or official of a constitutional body shall not perform the functions of their office as per Article 101(6) of the Constitution until such proceedings are concluded.
(9) The Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall inform the concerned bodies and officials about the suspension of duties as per Sub-rule (8) under the direction of the Speaker.

165. Impeachment Recommendation Committee: (1) There shall be an Impeachment Recommendation Committee in the House of Representatives to investigate whether the grounds and reasons for presenting a motion of impeachment against officials as per Article 101(2) of the Constitution exist and to make recommendations.
(2) The Impeachment Recommendation Committee shall consist of eleven members.
(3) The Speaker shall present a proposal for the formation of the Impeachment Recommendation Committee to the House for approval, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.
(4) The members of the Impeachment Recommendation Committee shall elect a chairperson from among themselves.

166. Procedure of Impeachment Recommendation Committee: (1) The Impeachment Recommendation Committee shall commence its proceedings as soon as possible after receiving the motion of impeachment.
(2) After commencing proceedings as per Sub-rule (1), the committee shall give the accused official a reasonable opportunity to present their defense within seven days and conduct necessary investigations.
(3) If the defense submitted as per Sub-rule (2) is not satisfactory, or if the official fails to appear and present any defense within the stipulated period, the Impeachment Recommendation Committee shall submit its report with recommendations to the House.
(4) The Impeachment Recommendation Committee shall submit its report with recommendations within a maximum of three months from the date of commencement of its proceedings.
(5) The committee shall manage its own internal procedures.

167. Report of Impeachment Recommendation Committee: (1) The Impeachment Recommendation Committee shall submit its report with recommendations to the House.
(2) After the report is presented as per Sub-rule (1), it shall be distributed to all members.

168. Consideration of Impeachment Recommendation Committee Report: (1) In the meeting held on the day and time specified by the Speaker after the report is presented as per Rule 160, the chairperson of the Impeachment Recommendation Committee, or any member of the committee designated by the Speaker in his absence, shall present a motion seeking consideration of the committee's report in the House.
(2) After the motion as per Sub-rule (1) is presented, the report shall be discussed in the meeting.
(3) During the discussion as per Sub-rule (2), any member may present a motion to send the report back to the Impeachment Recommendation Committee for further investigation on any specific matter.
(4) If the motion to send the report back to the Impeachment Recommendation Committee as per Sub-rule (3) is approved, the report shall be sent back to the committee, and the committee shall investigate again within seven days and submit its report to the meeting.

169. Motion to Approve Impeachment Recommendation Committee Report: (1) After the discussion on the report as per Rule 168(2) is concluded, or after the report of the Impeachment Recommendation Committee is received back in the meeting as per Rule 168(4), the chairperson of the committee, or any other member of the committee designated by the Speaker in his absence, may present a motion seeking approval of the recommendations mentioned in the report.
(2) After the motion as per Sub-rule (1) is presented, any member may present amendments to the report, if the Speaker deems appropriate.
(3) After a brief discussion on the motion presented as per Sub-rule (1) or the amendments presented as per Sub-rule (2), the Speaker shall present all amendments for decision to the House, and thereafter, the recommendations mentioned in the report shall be presented to the House for decision.
(4) The decision on the motion of impeachment shall be made by a division of votes with signatures of the members.
(5) If the motion of impeachment against the President or Vice-President as per this chapter is passed by a two-thirds majority of the total members currently present in the House, it shall be sent to the National Assembly with a message.
(6) Proceedings on a motion of impeachment as per Rule 164 shall be concluded by the House of Representatives within a maximum of five months.
(7) If a motion as per Rule 164 is submitted when the term of the House of Representatives is less than five months, the Speaker may decide to conclude the proceedings within the period determined by the Business Advisory Committee using an expedited procedure.
(8) If the proceedings cannot be concluded even through the expedited procedure adopted as per Sub-rule (10), or if the motion of impeachment remains on record, it shall be considered as a record for the new House of Representatives. The newly elected House of Representatives shall accept such a record within five months of its formation and make a necessary decision within the next five months.

170. Notwithstanding anything contained herein: Proceedings under this chapter shall be conducted in accordance with this chapter.

Chapter 20
Secret Meeting of the House

171. Secret Meeting of the House: (1) The Speaker may decide to hold a discussion on any matter of national security and national crisis in a secret meeting if the Prime Minister requests it and the Speaker deems the request appropriate.
(2) During a secret meeting of the House, no person other than the members of the House shall be allowed to be present in the meeting hall. However, members of the National Assembly and Secretariat staff, security personnel, or other individuals may be present in the meeting hall with the Speaker's permission.
(3) The Speaker may keep a brief record of the proceedings or decisions of a secret meeting and publish it in a manner deemed appropriate by him. No person other than the Speaker or a person authorized by him shall keep any record of the proceedings of a secret meeting or disclose any information about such a meeting to anyone.
(4) The procedure for conducting a secret meeting shall be as determined by the Speaker.
(5) If it is deemed no longer necessary to keep the proceedings of a secret meeting confidential, the Prime Minister or a member authorized by him may present a motion in the meeting to declassify the proceedings of the secret meeting.
(6) If the motion as per Sub-rule (5) is approved, the Speaker shall prepare a declassified summary of the proceedings and decisions of the secret meeting and publish it as determined.

Chapter 29
Committees of the House

172. Committees May Be Formed: The House of Representatives may form thematic committees to assist in its regular work and special committees as needed.

173. Thematic Committees: The following committees with the names and areas of work as specified below shall be formed in the House of Representatives as per Article 97 of the Constitution to assist the Government of Nepal, monitor and evaluate its work, provide necessary directives or advice, and be accountable and responsible to the House of Representatives:-

S.No. | Committee Name | Area of Work
---|---|---
1. | Finance Committee | Ministry of Finance, National Natural Resources and Fiscal Commission, Investment Board, National Planning Commission, and related ministries and bodies.
2. | International Relations and Tourism Committee | Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Ministry of Culture, Tourism and Civil Aviation, and related subjects and bodies.
3. | Social Justice and Human Rights Committee | Ministry of Labour, Employment and Social Security, Ministry of Industry, Commerce and Supplies, and related subjects and bodies.
4. | Law, Justice and Parliamentary Affairs Committee | Ministry of Law, Justice and Parliamentary Affairs, judicial administration and implementation, fundamental rights, treaties, international conventions, peace processes, office of the Attorney General, Judicial Council, Judicial Service Commission, and related subjects/bodies.
5. | Agriculture, Cooperatives and Natural Resources Committee | Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock Development, Ministry of Cooperatives and Poverty Alleviation, Ministry of Forests and Environment, and related subjects and bodies.
6. | Women, Children, Senior Citizens and Social Affairs Committee | Ministry of Women, Children and Senior Citizens, National Women Commission, National Dalit Commission, National Inclusion Commission, Madhesi Commission, Tharu Commission, Muslim Commission, social security, rights and interests of marginalized communities, and related subjects and bodies.
7. | State Management and Good Governance Committee | Office of the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, Ministry of Home Affairs, Ministry of Federal Affairs and General Administration, Commission for Investigation of Abuse of Authority, Public Service Commission, Election Commission, and related subjects and bodies.
8. | Infrastructure Development Committee | Ministry of Physical Infrastructure and Transport, Ministry of Energy, Water Resources and Irrigation, Ministry of Urban Development, and related subjects and bodies.
9. | Education, Science and Technology Committee | Ministry of Education, Science and Technology, Ministry of Health and Population, Ministry of Information and Communications Technology, Ministry of Youth and Sports, and related subjects and bodies.
10. | Public Accounts Committee | Public accounts, report of the Auditor General, and related subjects and bodies.

174. Additional Subjects to Be Specified: The Speaker may, if deemed appropriate, specify subjects not covered in Rule 173 within the scope of any committee, ensuring that no two committees deal with the same subject.
If any subject appears to fall within the jurisdiction of more than one committee, the Speaker shall consult with the chairpersons of the concerned committees.

175. Formation of Thematic Committees: (1) Each committee as per Rule 173 shall consist of a maximum of twenty-seven members, excluding ex-officio members, nominated by the Speaker with the consent of the House of Representatives.
(2) Members whose interests conflict with the committee's area of work shall not be nominated to the respective committee.

Clarification: For the purpose of this sub-rule, "conflicting interest" shall mean a subject matter with conflicting interests as per prevailing law. Similarly, discussions and decisions in the meeting shall be conducted as per Rule 194.
(3) The Prime Minister shall be an ex-officio member of all committees, and the minister concerned with the committee's area of work shall also be an ex-officio member of that committee.
(4) In nominating members to the committee, proportional representation shall be ensured based on the number of members of political parties represented in the House, as well as representation of women, Dalits, indigenous nationalities, Khas-Arya, Madhesi, Tharu, Muslim, backward communities, minorities, and marginalized communities.
(5) A member, other than a minister, shall not be a member of more than one thematic committee simultaneously.
(6) Notwithstanding anything contained in Sub-rule (5), if a member wishes to participate in the discussion of a committee of which they are not a member, they may do so by informing the chairperson of the concerned committee at least one day before the meeting. However, such a member shall not have the right to vote in that committee.

176. Election of Chairperson: (1) Unless otherwise provided in this chapter, the election of the chairperson of committees under this chapter shall be held on a day specified by the Speaker, and the Secretary shall publish the notice forty-eight hours in advance.
(2) The day before the date set for the election of the chairperson, by 4:00 PM, any member shall submit a proposal to the Secretary in the format of Schedule-3 to elect another member as chairperson.
(3) After the proposal is presented by the proposing member in the committee, the supporting members shall support it.
(4) If only one proposal is received as per Sub-rule (2), after the supporting members have spoken, the member presiding over the committee shall declare the proposed member elected as chairperson unopposed. If more than one proposal is received, after the supporting members have spoken in order, a general discussion shall be held on all proposals, and then the member presiding over the committee shall present the proposals to the committee for decision one by one.
(5) By asking members in favor to say "Aye" and members against to say "No", the member who secures a majority shall be declared elected as chairperson.
(6) If more than one proposal is presented for decision as per Sub-rule (4), the member proposed in the proposal that is approved shall be deemed elected as chairperson, and the remaining proposals shall not be presented for decision.
(7) The member presiding over the committee shall not have the right to vote. However, this sub-rule shall not prevent the presiding member from casting a casting vote in case of a tie.
(8) An ex-officio member shall have the right to vote in only one committee of which they are a member. Information about which committee they have voted in must be provided to the Speaker within twenty-four hours of the publication of the election notice. However, an ex-officio member who is not a member of the House of Representatives shall not have the right to vote.
(9) No minister shall be elected as chairperson and preside over a committee meeting.
(10) In the absence of the chairperson, the senior-most member present shall preside over the committee meeting.
(11) If the senior-most member is proposed for the post of chairperson, the next senior-most member among the present members shall preside over the committee meeting.
(12) The elected chairperson of the committee shall take an oath of office and secrecy before the Speaker in the format of Schedule-4.
(13) In the election of the chairperson under this chapter, the chairperson shall be elected on the basis of inclusivity, ensuring that at least one-third of the members of the thematic committee are women.
(14) Other procedures for the election of committee chairpersons shall be as determined by the Speaker.

177. Term of Thematic Committee and Vacancy of Chairperson and Member: (1) The term of each committee as per Rule 173 shall be for the duration of the House of Representatives.
However, the Speaker may, with the consent of the House of Representatives, make changes or additions to the members.
(2) The post of committee chairperson shall become vacant in any of the following circumstances:-
(a) If the member ceases to be a member,
(b) If the member becomes a minister,
(c) If the member resigns,
(d) If a proposal is passed by a two-thirds majority of the total members of the concerned committee stating that the member has not fulfilled their responsibilities with integrity.
(3) The post of committee member shall become vacant in any of the following circumstances:-
(a) If the member ceases to be a member,
(b) If the member becomes a minister,
(c) If the member resigns from the post of committee member,
(d) If the member ceases to be a member due to changes in membership as per Sub-rule (1).
(4) If the post of committee chairperson becomes vacant, an election shall be held within fifteen days from the date of vacancy as per Rule 176, and if the post of member becomes vacant, a nomination shall be made as per Rule 175(1).
(5) If the post of committee chairperson becomes vacant, an election shall be held within fifteen days from the date of vacancy.

178. Work, Duties, and Authority of Thematic Committee: (1) Within the scope of their respective areas of work as specified in Rule 173, the work, duties, and authority of each thematic committee shall be as follows:-
(a) To discuss bills falling within the committee's area of work clause by clause and submit a report thereon,
(b) To evaluate the policies and programs, resource mobilization, management, and other activities of the ministry, department, and subordinate bodies, provide necessary directives to the concerned bodies, and submit an annual report with appropriate comments, recommendations, and directives to the meeting,
(c) To examine the revenue and expenditure estimates of the ministry, department, and subordinate bodies, provide necessary directives to the concerned bodies regarding the method of preparing annual estimates, alternative policies that can be adopted regarding the policies contained in the annual estimates, and the extent of savings that can be made in the amount of the annual estimates, and submit an annual report with their opinions to the meeting,
(d) To study the steps taken by the Government of Nepal to fulfill the commitments and assurances given by ministers from time to time in the meeting, provide necessary directives to the concerned bodies, and submit a report to the meeting,
(e) To study, monitor, and evaluate the misappropriation or otherwise of government property related to its area of work, provide necessary directives, and submit a report to the meeting,
(f) To evaluate whether laws made under delegated authority by the House, relevant Acts, and prevailing laws are in accordance with the Constitution, relevant Acts, and rules, and whether the government is acting in accordance with prevailing laws and rules, and provide necessary directives to the concerned bodies,
(g) To monitor the implementation of reports submitted by inquiry commissions or committees and investigation commissions or committees formed by the government from time to time, and provide necessary advice, suggestions, and directives to the concerned bodies,
(h) To hear complaints received from the general public within its area of work,
(i) To conduct monitoring and evaluation of work performed by related ministries, departments, and other bodies, and provide necessary advice, suggestions, and directives,
(j) To undertake work related to the measurement of law implementation,
(k) To monitor and evaluate the work and performance of constitutional bodies within its area of work, provide reports, advice, suggestions, and directives, and discuss annual reports, providing suggestions,
(l) The concerned bodies shall provide progress reports on the implementation of directives, advice, suggestions, and directives given by the committee within thirty days.
(m) The committee may review the implementation of directives, advice, suggestions, and directives given by it within thirty days of their receipt.
(n) To monitor and evaluate the implementation of directives given by the committee and submit a report to the House,
(o) To discuss the annual reports of constitutional bodies within its area of work, provide advice, suggestions, and directives, and submit reports,
(p) To perform any other tasks assigned by the meeting,
(q) To conduct its activities within the framework of the budget and program approved by the Speaker. The committee's work shall not be hampered by lack of resources and means.
(2) While performing its work, the committee may consult with representatives of the concerned ministries and departments, commissions, and bodies, and with experts in the relevant field as needed.
(3) Representatives of the concerned bodies and experts invited by the committee as per Sub-rule (2) shall be obliged to appear before the committee and provide truthful information.
(4) The presence of ministers shall be mandatory during discussions on bills in the committee, and their presence shall be mandatory during discussions on other agenda items as needed.
(5) The concerned bodies shall be obliged to provide the committee with information on the status of implementation of directives and instructions given by the Speaker during the conduct of the House. If directives are not implemented, the concerned bodies shall provide the committee with clear reasons.
(6) Laws made under delegated legislation and related documents shall be made available within thirty days.
(7) The thematic committee may formulate and implement necessary work plans and schedules to effectively carry out its responsibilities within its area of work.
(8) The Public Accounts Committee may investigate the discrepancies mentioned in the annual report of the Auditor General submitted to the House, study whether the work performed by the Auditor General and related bodies has been done legally and justifiably, and provide necessary directives.
(9) During the course of its work, committee members may conduct field visits to necessary locations with the prior permission of the Speaker.
(10) When seeking permission for field visits as per Sub-rule (9), the reason for the field visit must be clearly stated.
(11) The committee may submit its annual report to the meeting at least fifteen days before the presentation of the annual estimates, and other reports in a timely manner.

179. Matters to Be Considered When Dealing with Delegated Legislation: (1) While discussing delegated legislation as per Rule 173, the committee shall consider the following matters:-
(a) Whether the rules made under delegated authority are in accordance with the objective of the Constitution or the Act,
(b) Whether the Government of Nepal or the body delegated authority by the Parliament has made delegated legislation as required by the Act,
(c) Whether the matters specified by the Act are included in the delegated legislation,
(d) Whether the delegated legislation includes provisions for imposing or collecting any tax,
(e) Whether the delegated legislation directly or indirectly interferes with the jurisdiction of the court,
(f) Whether the delegated legislation is retrospective or not, regarding any provision for which the Constitution or Act has not explicitly granted such authority,
(g) Whether any expenditure is to be incurred from the Contingency Fund or other government funds under the rules,
(h) Whether the delegated legislation is within the scope of authority granted by the Constitution or Act,
(i) Whether there is any unnecessary delay in publishing such delegated legislation and submitting it to the Secretariat,
(j) Whether any clarification is needed to understand the intent of such delegated legislation.
(2) When submitting a report to the House as per Sub-rule (1), the committee may also include the following matters:-
(a) Recommendations for the complete or partial repeal or amendment of any rule, along with reasons, if deemed necessary,
(b) Study of the steps taken by the Government of Nepal to fulfill the assurances given by ministers from time to time in the House or committee, and directives given to the concerned bodies.
(3) If it is necessary to inform the House about any other matter related to a rule while preparing the report as per Sub-rule (2), such matter shall also be disclosed in the report.
(4) Any member may present amendments to the report submitted as per Sub-rule (2), if the Speaker deems appropriate.
(5) The Speaker shall set the date and time for discussion on the report submitted by the committee and any amendments presented thereto.
(6) The concerned minister shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion on the report and amendments at the end of the discussion.
(7) After the decision on amendments, the recommendations of the report, as they are or with approved amendments, shall be presented to the House for decision.
(8) The Secretary shall inform the concerned minister of the decision as per Sub-rule (7).
(9) The concerned minister shall implement the recommendations approved by the House.
(10) The concerned minister shall inform the House about the implementation or repeal of the matters mentioned in the recommendations as per Sub-rule (9).

180. Special Committee: (1) Special committees may be formed in the House of Representatives as needed for special, sensitive, or serious bills or other tasks.
(2) If a special committee needs to be formed as per Sub-rule (1), the Speaker shall present a proposal to the House, including the work to be done by the committee, its term, and the list of members, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.

181. Committee Meeting: (1) Unless otherwise provided in this chapter, the chairperson of the concerned committee may convene a committee meeting as needed. However, if the committee meeting is adjourned, and if one-fourth of the committee members request the chairperson in writing to convene a meeting with the subject matter for discussion, the chairperson shall set the date and time for the meeting within five days.
(2) The meeting of the committee shall be presided over by the chairperson or a person chosen by them. If a person chosen by the members presides, the Speaker shall be informed of this.
(3) When a person chosen by the members presides over a committee meeting, they shall exercise all the powers that the committee chairperson can exercise.
(4) If a committee or sub-committee meeting needs to be held outside the Parliament building premises, the chairperson shall obtain written permission from the Speaker.

182. Quorum: (1) Unless otherwise provided in this chapter, the quorum for a committee shall be fifty-one percent of the total number of members currently present in the committee.
(2) If the quorum is not met in a committee meeting, the committee chairperson may adjourn the meeting until the quorum is met or adjourn it to another time or day.
(3) Even if the quorum is not met for a committee meeting convened as per Sub-rule (2), the meeting may be held with the presence of at least one-third of the members. The chairperson shall inform the Speaker of this.

183. Action for Absence: A member who remains absent from five consecutive committee meetings without informing the chairperson may be removed from the committee upon the recommendation of the chairperson, and the meeting shall be informed of this.

184. Decision of Committee: (1) Unless otherwise provided in this chapter, the decision of the committee shall be by a majority of the members present in the committee meeting, and in case of a tie, the chairperson shall cast the deciding vote.
(2) The decision of the committee meeting shall be authenticated by the chairperson and the committee secretary.

185. Formation of Sub-committee: (1) The committee may form sub-committees from among its members to conduct internal committee work or to study and report on matters within the committee's area of work.
(2) When forming a sub-committee, the work to be done by the sub-committee and the time limit for its completion shall be specified.
(3) If a member of a sub-committee formed as per Sub-rule (1) needs to conduct a field visit for the study of any subject, prior permission must be obtained through the chairperson.

186. Attendance of Concerned Official or Expert: (1) When discussing any matter in a committee or sub-committee, if the concerned minister deems it necessary, they may bring a concerned official or expert of the Government of Nepal to the committee with permission to provide clarification on the subject. It shall be the duty of such an official or expert to appear before the committee and provide necessary clarification.
(2) The committee may invite representatives of concerned parties or interest groups and experts to seek their opinions.
(3) It shall be the duty of representatives of concerned parties or interest groups and experts who appear before the committee as per Sub-rule (2) to provide necessary clarification.

187. Shortening Discussion: Regardless of whether a time limit has been set or not, if the chairperson deems the discussion in the committee to be unnecessarily prolonged, they may shorten the discussion.

188. Authority to Summon Documents and Make Concerned Persons or Government Officials Appear: (1) The committee may summon necessary documents for its work or summon concerned persons or government officials to appear.
However, if the minister deems that presenting such documents would be contrary to national security, peace and order, public interest, or national interest, they shall inform the concerned committee of the reasons. The committee's decision in this regard shall be final.
(2) The committee may question or cause to be questioned any person or government official summoned as per Sub-rule (1) if necessary.

189. Committee Report: (1) The chairperson of the committee, or any other member designated by the chairperson in his absence, shall present the committee's report to the meeting.
(2) The chairperson or the member may give a brief statement regarding the report when presenting it as per Sub-rule (1).
(3) The concerned minister shall implement the reports presented to the meeting, and shall regularly inform the committee about the status of implementation.
(4) The committee may monitor and evaluate the implementation of its annual report and submit a report to the meeting.
(5) The chairperson of the concerned committee may, within two days of the presentation of the report as per Sub-rule (4), submit a notice to the Speaker seeking discussion on the report on monitoring and evaluation.
(6) If a notice is received as per Sub-rule (5), the Speaker shall grant permission to the chairperson of the concerned committee to present a motion for discussion in any meeting held two days thereafter.
(7) When presenting the motion, the chairperson of the concerned committee may give a brief statement. Thereafter, the Speaker shall set a time limit for the discussion.
(8) After the concerned minister responds to the questions raised during the discussion, the discussion shall be deemed concluded.

190. Authority to Maintain Order and Discipline in Committee: (1) The Speaker shall have all the powers vested in the Speaker regarding maintaining peace, order, and discipline in the meeting hall and conducting the committee's meetings smoothly, as per this regulation. However, if a member is expelled or suspended from the committee, it shall not be considered as expulsion or suspension from the meeting or any other committee of the House of Representatives.
(2) If a member is expelled or suspended as per Sub-rule (1), the chairperson shall inform the Speaker of this.

191. Notice of Absence: A member who remains absent from five consecutive committee meetings shall inform the chairperson of the reason for their absence, and for absences exceeding this period, permission must be obtained from the Speaker.

192. Committee Secretariat: (1) There shall be a secretariat for each committee under the Secretariat.
(2) The Secretary shall be the ex-officio secretary of each committee.
(3) The Secretary General shall appoint an officer of at least Gazetted First Class rank to serve as the secretary of the committee.
(4) The committee secretary may seek necessary information from the concerned ministry, department, or body to assist in the committee's work.

193. Secretary, etc., May Give Opinions: The Secretary General, Secretary, and Committee Secretary may give opinions on matters under consideration in a committee meeting with the permission of the chairperson.

194. Member with Direct Personal Interest Shall Not Participate in Discussion and Decision: A member whose direct personal interest or benefit is linked to a matter under consideration by a committee shall not participate in the discussion and decision on that matter.

195. Determination of Committee's Jurisdiction: (1) A committee shall discuss, study, and monitor only matters falling within its designated jurisdiction.
(2) If a situation arises where a committee needs to discuss, study, or monitor a matter falling within the jurisdiction of another committee, prior permission must be obtained from the Speaker.
(3) If a dispute arises regarding the jurisdiction of a committee, the Speaker's decision shall be final.

196. Meeting of Committee Chairpersons: To evaluate the effectiveness and work of the committees, a meeting of committee chairpersons may be convened from time to time under the chairmanship of the Speaker. The committee secretary may also participate in such meetings.

Chapter 22
Privileges Committee

197. Formation of Privileges Committee: (1) A Privileges Committee shall exist in the House of Representatives as per Article 103 of the Constitution. The Speaker may nominate a maximum of fifteen members to such a committee in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.
However, no minister shall be a member of the Privileges Committee.
(2) In nominating members to the Privileges Committee, proportional representation shall be ensured, taking into account the knowledge, experience, and the party, gender, ethnic, and regional composition of the House of Representatives, ensuring representation of all political parties represented in the House.
(3) The members of the Privileges Committee shall elect a chairperson from among themselves.
(4) The term of the chairperson and members of the committee shall be for the duration of the House.
(5) The elected chairperson of the committee shall take an oath of office and secrecy before the Speaker in the format of Schedule-4.

198. Rules Applicable: Regarding the circumstances of vacancy of the post of chairperson or member of the Privileges Committee, the arrangement for filling the vacancy, the provision for the senior-most member to preside, quorum, committee meetings, and decisions, the rules of Chapter 21 shall apply as needed.

199. Notice of Breach of Privilege: (1) If any member feels that their privilege has been breached, they shall submit a written notice to the Secretary, stating the reasonable grounds for the breach of privilege. If such notice is based on any document, the document itself must be submitted, and if the document cannot be obtained, the location of the breach of privilege and the document shall be clearly stated in the notice.
(2) The Secretary shall promptly inform the Speaker and the chairperson of the Privileges Committee about the notice received as per Sub-rule (1), and a meeting shall be convened as directed by the chairperson.

200. Condition for Raising Breach of Privilege Issue: An issue of breach of privilege must relate to an event that occurred after the previous meeting concluded or a direct event that occurred during the current meeting.

201. Preliminary Investigation of Breach of Privilege Issue: (1) The Privileges Committee shall conduct a preliminary investigation into the issue of breach of privilege.
(2) During the preliminary investigation, the committee may conduct general inquiries with the concerned person and request the submission of documentary evidence.
(3) If, during the preliminary investigation, the Privileges Committee deems it reasonable to raise the issue of breach of privilege at first sight, it shall submit a preliminary report to the meeting with the reasons, and inform the Speaker and the concerned member whether to grant permission to raise the issue of breach of privilege.

202. Method of Raising Breach of Privilege Issue: (1) A member wishing to raise an issue of breach of privilege may, after the report of the Privileges Committee is presented, seek permission from the Speaker and present the matter briefly.
(2) If any member objects to the breach of privilege issue being brought up for discussion, the Speaker shall order the members to stand up. If twenty-five or more members, excluding the members of the Privileges Committee, stand up, the motion shall be deemed to have received permission from the House, and if fewer members stand up, the motion shall be deemed not to have received permission from the House.

203. Referral of Issue to Privileges Committee: (1) If permission is granted by the House to raise an issue of breach of privilege as per Rule 202, any member may present a motion to discuss the issue in the House or to send it to the Privileges Committee.
(2) After the motion as per Sub-rule (1) is presented, the Speaker shall set a time limit for the discussion on the motion.
(3) After the discussion as per Sub-rule (2) is concluded, the proposing member may, with the permission of the House, withdraw the motion. If the motion is not withdrawn, the Speaker shall present the motion to the House for decision.
(4) If the motion to discuss the issue of breach of privilege in the House is approved as per Sub-rule (3), the House shall decide on the issue, using the powers mentioned in Sub-rules (3) and (4) of Rule 204 as needed. The Speaker may adopt any appropriate procedure in this regard.
(5) If the motion to send the issue of breach of privilege to the committee is approved, the Speaker shall send the issue along with the proposal to the committee for investigation and submission of a report.

204. Procedure and Authority of Privileges Committee: (1) After receiving the proposal and documents as per Rule 203(5), the Privileges Committee shall commence its proceedings as soon as possible.
(2) The chairperson of the Privileges Committee shall manage the committee's proceedings under the direction of the Speaker.
(3) The Privileges Committee may order the arrest of the accused person during the investigation, summon witnesses, record evidence, and take depositions.
(4) The Privileges Committee may detain the accused person for a period of up to three months if deemed necessary. If the committee detains anyone, it shall inform the meeting accordingly. If the meeting rescinds such an order, the detained person shall be released immediately.

205. Report of Privileges Committee: (1) The Privileges Committee shall investigate each issue assigned to it, give the accused person a reasonable opportunity to present their defense, consider all facts, and submit a report with recommendations to the meeting, indicating whether a breach of privilege has occurred or not, along with the grounds and reasons.
(2) A copy of the report as per Sub-rule (1) shall be distributed to all members at least one day before it is presented to the meeting.

206. Consideration of Privileges Committee Report: (1) After the Privileges Committee report is presented to the meeting, the chairperson of the Privileges Committee, or the senior-most member of the committee in his absence, shall present a motion seeking consideration of the report in the meeting on the day and time specified by the Speaker.
(2) After the motion seeking consideration of the report is presented as per Sub-rule (1), the report shall be discussed in the meeting.

207. Amendment May Be Presented: (1) During the discussion on the report as per Rule 206(2), any member may present an amendment seeking to send the report back to the Privileges Committee for further investigation on any specific matter.
(2) After a brief discussion on the amendment presented as per Sub-rule (1), it shall be presented to the House for decision.
(3) If the amendment seeking to send the report back to the Privileges Committee is approved, the report shall be sent back to the committee. After re-investigation within seven days, the committee shall submit its report to the meeting.

208. Motion to Approve Privileges Committee Report: (1) After the discussion on the report of the Privileges Committee as per Rule 206(2) is concluded, or after the report is received back in the meeting as per Rule 207(3), the chairperson of the committee, or any other member of the committee designated by the Speaker in his absence, may present a motion seeking approval of the recommendations mentioned in the report.
(2) After the motion as per Sub-rule (1) is presented, any member may present amendments to the report, if the Speaker deems appropriate.
(3) After a brief discussion on the motion presented as per Sub-rule (1) or the amendments presented as per Sub-rule (2), the Speaker shall present all amendments for decision to the House, and thereafter, the recommendations mentioned in the report shall be presented to the House for decision.

209. Mercy or Reduction of Punishment May Be Granted: (1) In a breach of privilege case, the convicted person may submit a petition for mercy through the Speaker as per Article 103(8) of the Constitution.
(2) The Speaker shall present the petition for mercy and the convicted person's apology to the House. If the House is satisfied, it may grant a pardon or reduce the sentence imposed.

Chapter 23
Motion Regarding Speaker or Deputy Speaker Not Behaving Appropriately

210. Motion of Inappropriate Conduct: (1) If any member feels that the Speaker or Deputy Speaker has not behaved appropriately in their position, they may, with the support of one-fourth of the total members currently present in the House of Representatives, submit a notice of motion to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary, as per Article 91(6)(c) of the Constitution of Nepal.
(2) After the motion is received as per Sub-rule (1), the Secretary shall submit it to the Deputy Speaker if it is against the Speaker, to the Speaker if it is against the Deputy Speaker, and to the Senior Member as per Article 91(5) of the Constitution if it is against both the Speaker and the Deputy Speaker. The date and time for discussion on such a motion shall be set within seven days of its registration.
(3) At least one day before the day set for discussion on the motion as per Sub-rule (1), a copy of the motion shall be distributed to all members.

211. Presiding over the Meeting: If the motion as per Rule 210 is against the Speaker, the Deputy Speaker shall preside over the meeting on the day set for discussion on that motion. If the motion is against both the Speaker and the Deputy Speaker, the Senior Member as per Article 91(5) of the Constitution shall preside.

212. Discussion and Decision on Motion: (1) On the date and time set for discussion, the proposing member shall present the motion after their name is called by the person presiding over the House. If they wish, they may give a statement before presenting the motion. The official accused shall also have the right to participate in the discussion and vote.
(2) After the motion is presented as per Sub-rule (1), the person presiding over the House shall set a time limit for the discussion, and at the end of that period, the Speaker or Deputy Speaker accused of inappropriate conduct shall give a statement regarding their defense. After giving the statement regarding the defense, the proposing member may, with the permission of the House, withdraw the motion. If the proposing member does not wish to withdraw the motion, the motion shall be presented to the House for decision.
(3) The decision on the motion as per Sub-rule (2) shall be made by a division of votes with signatures of the members.
(4) If the motion as per Rule 210 is passed by a two-thirds majority of the total members currently present in the House, the concerned Speaker or Deputy Speaker shall be removed from office.

Chapter 24
Notice of Absence, Resignation, and Vacancy of Seat

213. Notice of Absence: (1) If a member is unable to attend ten consecutive meetings due to illness or unavoidable circumstances as per Article 89(d) of the Constitution, they shall provide advance notice to the House through the Speaker.
(2) If a member is unable to attend fewer than ten meetings of the House, they shall provide advance notice to the Speaker. If it is not possible to provide advance notice for a reasonable reason, notice shall be given within three days of their return.
(3) The notice provided as per Sub-rule (1) or (2) shall clearly state the period and reason for absence. Such notice may also be given through electronic means.
(4) The Speaker shall present the notice as per Sub-rule (1) to the House for approval, and the Speaker shall approve the notice as per Sub-rule (2).

214. Resignation: (1) The Speaker may submit their resignation to the Deputy Speaker, and the Deputy Speaker or any member may submit their resignation to the Speaker.
(2) The resignation submitted as per Sub-rule (1) shall be read out in the meeting by the person presiding over the meeting after verification. If the meeting is not in session, the resignation shall be published in the notice paper.

215. Notice of Vacancy of Seat: (1) If a member's seat becomes vacant as per Article 89 of the Constitution, the Speaker shall inform the House.
(2) If the session of the House is not in progress, the Speaker shall cause such information to be published in the notice paper.
(3) The Secretary shall provide the information regarding the vacancy of the seat as per Sub-rule (1) to the Council of Ministers and the Election Commission, and a notice regarding this shall be published in the Nepal Gazette.

Chapter 24
Parliamentary Party Office

216. Parliamentary Party Office: (1) The office of the parliamentary party of a political party represented in the House of Representatives shall be located within the Secretariat premises.
(2) Other arrangements regarding the operation of the parliamentary party office shall be managed by the Secretariat under the direction of the Speaker, based on the number of members in the party and in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.

217. Members to Submit Declaration Form: Each member of the House of Representatives shall submit a personal details form in the format of Schedule-5 within seven days from the date this regulation comes into force. However, members who have already submitted a personal details form before the commencement of this regulation need not submit it again.

218. Political Parties Represented in the House of Representatives to Provide Details: (1) The leader of a political party represented in the House of Representatives shall provide the following details to the Secretariat within seven days from the date this regulation comes into force:-
(a) Name and details of the members of their party,
(b) Names and specimen signatures of up to two members authorized by the party to correspond with the Secretariat, in the format of Schedule-6.
(c) A copy of the constitution or rules of the concerned political party.
(d) A copy of the constitution or rules of the concerned parliamentary party, if any.

Explanation: For the purpose of this chapter, "Leader of the Party" shall mean the member chosen by the party as its leader, and shall also include a member authorized by the leader to perform the functions of the leader in the House.
(2) If a party is formed during the term of the House, the leader of that party shall provide the details as per Sub-rule (1) to the Secretariat within seven days of its formation.
(3) If there is any change in the details provided as per Sub-rule (1), the leader of the party shall provide the details of the change to the Secretariat within seven days of the change.

Chapter 26
Member Conduct and Monitoring Arrangements

219. Code of Conduct: (1) To fulfill the responsibilities mandated by the Constitution and to maintain public trust in their work, the officials and members of the House of Representatives shall adhere to the following code of conduct:-
(a) To achieve the objectives of the House and fully comply with this regulation for that purpose,
(b) To behave in a manner consistent with public morality and conduct prevalent in society,
(c) To attend meetings or committees of which they are members, participate regularly, and exercise their voting rights in their work,
(d) To perform their duties impartially, honestly, and responsibly,
(e) To prioritize public interest in all activities related to the meeting and to stand for public interest in situations where private interest conflicts with public interest,
(f) Not to speak, vote, or participate in any activity of the meeting in exchange for personal financial gain, fees, remuneration, or rewards,
(g) To use confidential information and data obtained as a member only for the purpose of the meeting and not to use it as a subject of transaction with any other person or organization for financial gain,
(h) To disclose to the House or committee if their personal or private interest is involved in any matter under consideration in the House or a committee of which they are a member, and to refrain from participating in the discussion on that matter.
(2) The code of conduct under this rule shall apply to all aspects of the public life of officials and members. However, it shall not be considered to regulate the personal or private life of officials and members in its entirety.

220. Formation of Conduct Monitoring Committee: (1) There shall be a Conduct Monitoring Committee in the House of Representatives.
(2) The committee as per Sub-rule (1) shall be constituted as follows:-
(a) Chairperson: Speaker
(b) Vice-Chairperson: Deputy Speaker
(c) Member: Leader of the Opposition Party
(d) Member: A maximum of twelve members nominated by the Speaker based on proportional representation of political parties represented in the House of Representatives, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.
(3) The Secretary General of the Federal Parliament shall serve as the secretary of the committee.

221. Work, Duties, and Authority of Conduct Monitoring Committee: (1) The work, duties, and authority of the Conduct Monitoring Committee shall be as follows:-
(a) To conduct necessary investigations based on public issues raised regarding any member's violation of the code of conduct mentioned in Rule 219(1) or on information provided by anyone to the committee,
(b) To obtain necessary information from the concerned member and other persons regarding issues of conduct,
(c) To obtain necessary information from the person, organization, or media raising issues of conduct,
(d) To clearly determine, based on facts, whether the concerned member has violated conduct during the investigation of issues related to conduct, and submit a report thereon to the meeting.
(2) The committee may form sub-committees with experts as needed for conduct monitoring.
(3) The Conduct Monitoring Committee shall determine its own procedures.
(4) It shall be the duty of a member to cooperate fully if an investigation into a violation of conduct has been initiated against them under this rule.
(5) If an investigation into a violation of conduct has been initiated against a member under this rule, the concerned member shall be given an opportunity to present their defense to the Conduct Monitoring Committee.
(6) The report submitted by the Conduct Monitoring Committee to the meeting as per Sub-rule (1)(d) shall be kept in the records of the meeting. A copy of the report shall be sent to the office of the parliamentary party of the concerned member.

Chapter 27
Statement by Minister, Tabling of Documents, and Resignation

222. Statement and Information by Minister: (1) A minister may make a statement or provide information in the meeting with the Speaker's permission on matters of public importance.
(2) Any member may ask questions related to the subject matter of the statement or information provided as per Sub-rule (1) with the Speaker's permission, and the concerned minister shall answer such questions.

223. Documents to Be Tabled: (1) If a minister or proposing member quotes any government document or report, or part thereof, while making a statement or providing information in the meeting, such document or report shall be tabled in the meeting.
However, if the minister deems that presenting such a document or report would be contrary to national security, peace and order, public interest, or national interest, they shall inform the House of the reasons and may refrain from tabling such a document or report.
(2) If only a summary of a document or report is mentioned, such a document or report need not be tabled.

224. Statement by Resigning Minister: (1) A member resigning from the post of minister may, with the Speaker's permission, make a statement in the meeting regarding their resignation.
(2) There shall be no discussion on the statement made as per Sub-rule (1).

Chapter 28
Treaty

225. Presentation of Treaty: (1) If ratification, accession, acceptance, or endorsement of a treaty is required as per Article 279 of the Constitution, the concerned minister may present a proposal for such treaty in any meeting held seven days after giving notice, along with the nature of the treaty and the following details:-
(a) Objective of the treaty, its main provisions, and the reasons and justification for Nepal being a party to such a treaty,
(b) National interest to be served by such a treaty and the responsibilities to be fulfilled,
(c) Whether new laws need to be enacted or institutional and other arrangements need to be made to implement such a treaty, and if so, details thereof,
(d) In the case of a multilateral treaty, the names and number of countries that are already parties to such a treaty,
(e) Any interpretation or opinion expressed by the contracting party regarding the treaty,
(f) Reasons for reservations, if any, to be made in a multilateral treaty, and the proposal for such reservations.
(2) After receiving the notice as per Sub-rule (1), the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall make a copy of the treaty available to the members at least five days before the meeting where it is to be presented.
(3) The concerned minister shall give a statement while presenting the proposal for ratification, accession, acceptance, or endorsement of the treaty in the meeting.
(4) After the minister gives a statement as per Sub-rule (3), the Speaker shall set a time limit for general discussion on the treaty.

226. General Discussion on Treaty: (1) In any meeting following the presentation of a treaty as per Rule 225, the proposing member shall present a motion seeking general discussion on the treaty.
(2) During the discussion on the motion as per Sub-rule (1), discussion shall be limited to whether ratification, accession, acceptance, or endorsement is required or not, and only on the theoretical aspect of the treaty.
(3) At the end of the discussion, the concerned minister shall respond to the questions raised.

227. Amendment Regarding Reservations: (1) Within seventy-two hours after the conclusion of the general discussion as per Rule 226, if reservations can be made under the provisions of the treaty, or if the Government of Nepal has clarified its position on the interpretation of any provision, or if such a proposal has been made, members wishing to present an amendment limited to such matters shall submit notice thereof to the Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary.
(2) The Speaker shall have the authority to approve, reject, or call the concerned member to improve any amendment received as per Sub-rule (1), or to consolidate amendments of similar intent and approve them, subject to this regulation.
(3) If there are no reservations made under the provisions of a treaty, no amendment shall be presented on such a treaty.

228. Procedure After General Discussion is Concluded: In any meeting after the general discussion on a treaty is concluded, the proposing minister may present one of the following motions:-
(a) That the provisions of the treaty be discussed in the House,
(b) That the treaty be sent to the concerned committee for discussion.

229. Detailed Discussion on Provisions of Treaty with Amendments: (1) When discussing the provisions of a treaty with amendments, discussion shall generally be held on reservations in order, and the member whose name is called by the Speaker or their authorized representative may present the amendment.
(2) The Speaker shall present each provision of the treaty, whether unchanged or with amendments accepted, to the House for decision in order, stating "Let this provision be part of the treaty".
However, for the sake of time efficiency, discussion on multiple provisions or interdependent amendments under a provision may be allowed to be held together, and parts or provisions of the treaty may be deferred for discussion later or brought forward for discussion later.

230. Amendment May Be Withdrawn: The proposing member of an amendment may withdraw the amendment with the permission of the House.

231. Committee Report: After the detailed discussion on the provisions of the treaty is concluded in the concerned committee, the chairperson of the committee, or any other member designated by the chairperson in his absence, shall prepare and present the committee's report to the House.

232. Procedure After Committee Report is Presented: (1) After the committee's report is presented, it shall be distributed to all members.
(2) Unless the Speaker orders otherwise, in any meeting held twenty-four hours after the distribution of the report, the proposing member of the treaty may present a motion seeking discussion on the treaty along with the report.
(3) If the motion as per Sub-rule (2) is approved, discussion shall be held only on the provisions of the treaty and related schedules as per the committee's report.

233. Motion to Ratify, Accede, Accept, or Endorse Treaty: (1) After the detailed discussion on the treaty is concluded in the meeting, and after the discussion on the committee's report is concluded and amendments are presented to the House for decision one by one, the proposing member of the treaty shall present a motion to ratify the treaty.
(2) Ratification, accession, acceptance, or endorsement of treaties mentioned in Article 279(2) of the Constitution shall require approval by a two-thirds majority of the total members currently present in the House of Representatives.
(3) Ratification, accession, acceptance, or endorsement of treaties mentioned in clauses (a) and (d) of Article 279(2) of the Constitution, which are of a general nature, less serious, or long-term in impact on the nation, shall be by a simple majority of the members present in the meeting of the House of Representatives.
(4) After the proposal for ratification, accession, acceptance, or endorsement of a treaty is approved as per Sub-rule (2), the Secretary shall send a message with the approved treaty to the House.
(5) When a message is received after a treaty mentioned in Article 279(2) of the Constitution, passed by the National Assembly and sent to the House, is passed by the National Assembly, the Secretary shall table it in the House, and a copy shall be distributed to the members.
(6) Two days after the distribution of the copy of the treaty along with the message as per Sub-rule (5), the proposing member may present a motion seeking consideration thereof. However, the Speaker may permit such a motion to be presented in any meeting after the distribution of the copy of the treaty with the message, if deemed appropriate.
(7) The procedure following the presentation of the motion as per Sub-rule (6) shall be the same as the procedure for treaties presented in the House.

234. Information to Be Sent: The Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall inform the concerned ministry and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs whether a treaty has been ratified, acceded to, accepted, or endorsed as per Rule 233.

Chapter 29
Arrangements Regarding Declaration or Order of State of Emergency

235. Proposal for Approval of Declaration or Order of State of Emergency: (1) If the President issues a declaration or order of a state of emergency in Nepal as per Article 273(5) of the Constitution, the Prime Minister shall submit the proposal for approval of such declaration or order to the Secretary General within one month from the date of issuance of the order, as per Article 273(3) of the Constitution.
(2) After receiving the notice of the declaration or order as per Sub-rule (1), the Speaker shall set the date and time for presenting the proposal in the first meeting held thereafter.
(3) On the date and time specified as per Sub-rule (2), the Prime Minister shall, with the Speaker's permission, give a statement regarding the grounds and reasons for issuing the declaration or order of a state of emergency and present the motion to the House.
(4) After the motion is presented as per Sub-rule (3), the Speaker shall set a time limit for the discussion on the motion. At the end of the discussion, the Prime Minister or a minister authorized by him, if the Prime Minister is unable to be present due to illness or other unavoidable circumstances, shall respond to the questions raised during the discussion.
(5) After the discussion concludes as per Sub-rule (4), the Speaker shall present the motion to the House for decision.
(6) The decision on the motion shall be made by a division of votes with signatures of the members.
(7) If the session of the House of Representatives is not in progress at the time of issuance of the declaration or order as per Sub-rule (1), a session shall be convened within a maximum of fifteen days from the date of issuance of such declaration or order.

236. Proposal to Extend Term of Declaration or Order of State of Emergency: (1) The Prime Minister shall submit a proposal to extend the term of the declaration or order of a state of emergency to the Secretary General as per Article 273(6) of the Constitution.
(2) Other procedures related to the proposal as per Sub-rule (1) shall be as mentioned in Rule 234.

237. Information to Be Provided to Council of Ministers: The Secretary General or, in his absence, the Secretary shall inform the Council of Ministers whether the declaration or order as per this chapter has been approved or not.

Chapter 30
Miscellaneous

238. Attorney General to Express Opinion: (1) If the Speaker deems it necessary to seek the opinion of the Attorney General on any legal question, the Speaker shall allow the Attorney General to be present in the House and express their opinion as per Article 158(4) of the Constitution.
(2) There shall be no discussion on the opinion expressed as per Sub-rule (1).
(3) The procedure for expressing an opinion as per Sub-rule (1) shall be as determined by the Speaker.

239. Secretary General May Attend: The Secretary General may attend meetings of the House or any of its committees or sub-committees, provide advice to the House, committee, or sub-committee upon request, and direct the secretary or officer working in such meeting.

240. Security of Records: (1) The Secretary shall ensure the secure keeping of records of decisions and proceedings of the House and committees.
(2) Records related to the House and committees shall be kept in the Secretariat's library for study purposes.
(3) If a member requests a record of their speech and visuals, the Secretariat shall provide such a record.

241. Language of Proceedings: (1) The proceedings of the House of Representatives or its committees shall be conducted in the Nepali language. Any member may use a language recommended by the Language Commission and decided by the Government of Nepal, and arrangements shall be made to keep a record thereof.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in Sub-rule (1), in the case of members with disabilities in speaking, the Speaker may allow such members to express their views in sign language, and their accompanying person shall translate and read out such views in the meeting.

242. Dress and Emblem: (1) The dress of members shall be as determined by the Speaker in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee.
(2) While determining the dress code as per Sub-rule (1), attention shall be paid to formal, modest attire that reflects indigenous culture and tradition or identity.
(3) The emblem worn by members shall be as specified in Schedule-7. Such an emblem shall be respected.

243. Arrangements Regarding Secretariat: (1) A Secretariat shall exist as per Article 107 of the Constitution to assist in the conduct of proceedings and work of the Federal Parliament, to manage legislative affairs, to provide necessary services and facilities to officials and members, and to manage and operate the Federal Parliament Secretariat.
(2) Other arrangements regarding the Secretariat shall be in accordance with the Constitution, prevailing laws of Nepal, and the rules approved by the Secretariat Management Committee.

244. Arrangement Regarding Experts or Expert Groups: (1) The committee may, as per its decision, invite experts or expert groups, on a voluntary or paid basis, by specifying the terms of service, to provide consultation.
(2) The arrangements regarding the service facilities of invited experts on a paid basis as per Sub-rule (1) shall be determined by the operating directive prepared by the Secretariat.

245. Meetings May Be Held Using Technology: (1) If at least one-fourth of the members of the House agree that it is not possible to hold a meeting due to natural disasters or pandemics, and inform the Speaker accordingly, the Speaker may, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee, decide to hold meetings using technology as needed.
(2) If a committee meeting needs to be held as per Sub-rule (1), the chairperson of the concerned committee shall obtain written permission from the Speaker.
(3) The procedure for meetings held as per Sub-rules (1) and (2) shall be as determined by the Speaker.

246. Legal Opinion: If any legal question arises during the proceedings of the House or any of its committees or sub-committees, the legal advisor available at the Secretariat, along with six legal experts, may be consulted, and they shall represent the House or committee in any legal case where the House or committee is a party. However, this rule shall not prevent the House or committee from seeking assistance from separate legal experts for that purpose.

247. Information Regarding Arrest of Member: (1) If information is received regarding the arrest of any member, the Speaker shall immediately inform the House, subject to the proviso to Article 103(6) of the Constitution.
(2) If information of arrest is received when the meeting is not in session, the Speaker shall cause the information to be posted on the Secretariat's notice board for public information.
(3) If a member is arrested in connection with a criminal case punishable by imprisonment for three years or more or involving moral turpitude, and a charge sheet has been filed, and the member is in custody, or if the member is absconding while required to be in custody by court order, such a member shall not be entitled to perform any function or exercise any right or immunity as a member during the period of custody. During such a period, the member shall be suspended, and their salary, service, and facilities shall be suspended.

Clarification: For the purpose of this sub-rule, "Court" shall mean a District Court, High Court, and Supreme Court.
(4) If a member is convicted by a court in a criminal case and sentenced to imprisonment, or if a member is serving a sentence of imprisonment, or if a member is absconding after arrest and has not yet served the sentence, such a member shall be automatically deemed suspended for the duration of the imprisonment or until they are apprehended and serve the sentence. The member shall not be entitled to perform any function or exercise any right or immunity as a member during such a period, and their salary, service, and facilities shall be suspended during such a period.
(5) If a member arrested as per Sub-rule (3) is acquitted or released from custody, the Speaker shall immediately inform the House of the suspension and release. If the session is not in progress, the information shall be published in the notice paper. Upon suspension and release, the suspended salary, service, and facilities of such a member shall be reinstated.

248. Nomination of Member: Except as otherwise provided in this regulation, the Speaker may nominate members to committees, groups, organizations, or similar bodies related to the House of Representatives, considering the member's knowledge, skill, interest, and the party structure in the House. The formation and procedures of such committees, groups, organizations, or bodies shall be as determined by the Speaker.

249. Authority to Regulate Entry: (1) The meeting of the House shall generally be open to the public.
(2) The Speaker shall have the authority to regulate the entry of visitors to the meeting hall, keeping in mind their convenience. The Speaker may arrange for the issuance of entry passes as needed.
(3) Persons entering the meeting hall with an entry pass issued as per Sub-rule (2) shall abide by the conditions specified in the pass. If any person violates such conditions, they may be expelled from the meeting hall by order of the Speaker.

250. Address to the House: (1) The Speaker may grant permission for the head of state, head of government, or speaker of parliament of a foreign country to address the House upon the request of the Government of Nepal.
Such a dignitary addressing the House may be seated to the right of the Speaker's seat.
(2) The procedure to be followed for the address as per Sub-rule (1) shall be as determined by the Speaker.

251. Suspension of Rule: (1) Any member may present a motion to the House of Representatives seeking the suspension of any rule of this regulation for a specific period concerning any matter under consideration by the House. If such a motion is approved, the rule shall be deemed suspended for the time being.
(2) A motion as per Sub-rule (1) may generally be presented only for the purpose of shortening the time limit for passing a bill through a simplified procedure.

252. Brief Record of Proceedings: A brief record of the proceedings of the House shall be distributed to the members.

253. Format to Be Provided: The formats mentioned in various places in this regulation shall be provided by the Secretariat.

254. Work Schedule May Be Prepared and Implemented: The House shall prepare and implement a work schedule in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee and its committees to ensure the systematic conduct of business.

255. Consultation May Be Sought: Committees and officials may seek opinions from members of the House of Representatives, former members of the House, or former ministers during their work.

256. Information Flow: The flow of information regarding the House's proceedings shall be managed, including meetings with journalists and lobby reporting.

257. Internal Procedures: The Speaker may, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee and with the approval of the House, formulate internal procedures as needed.

258. Authority to Remove Obstacles: The Speaker shall have the authority to remove any obstacles encountered in the application of this regulation and to issue necessary orders or directives subject to the Constitution and this regulation. Such orders or directives shall require approval from the subsequent meeting. Orders or directives approved in this manner shall be considered equivalent to provisions in this regulation.

259. Regulation to Apply: (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in any prevailing law, this regulation shall apply as a special federal law to the House, committees, and members.
(2) This regulation shall serve as the privilege of the members of the House of Representatives.

260. Interpretation of Regulation: The Speaker shall have the authority to interpret this regulation, and their decision shall be final. Information about such a decision shall be provided to the House.

261. Internal Proceedings Deemed Valid: Internal proceedings conducted in accordance with this regulation shall be considered internal proceedings of the House and committees. No questions shall be raised in court regarding these.

262. Procedure for Amendment: (1) If any member deems it necessary to amend this regulation, they shall submit a proposal to the Speaker with the reasons and details.
(2) If the Speaker deems the notice as per Sub-rule (1) appropriate, the proposing member shall present a proposal to the House for approval to form a committee to draft the amendment to the regulation.
(3) The committee formed as per Sub-rule (2) shall consist of members as needed, in consultation with the Business Advisory Committee, and the chairperson of the committee shall be elected from among the members of the committee.
(4) After the committee submits its report, the subsequent procedure shall be the same as that adopted for bills, unless amended by the Speaker.

263. Repeal and Savings: (1) The House of Representatives Rules, 2079, is hereby repealed.
(2) Any work done under the House of Representatives Rules, 2079, shall be deemed to have been done under this regulation.

Internal Procedures of the House of Representatives Rules Drafting Committee, 2082

The committee has formulated these internal procedures to manage the proceedings of the House of Representatives Rules Drafting Committee formed by the meeting of the House of Representatives on 2082 Chaitra 23.

1. Short Title and Commencement: (1) The title of this procedure shall be "Internal Procedures of the House of Representatives Rules Drafting Committee, 2082".
(2) This procedure shall come into force immediately.

2. Definitions: Unless the subject or context otherwise requires, in this procedure:-
(a) "Secretary" shall mean the secretary of the committee.
(b) "Secretariat" shall mean the Federal Parliament Secretariat.
(c) "Member" shall mean a member of the House of Representatives Rules Drafting Committee.
(d) "House" shall mean the House of Representatives.
(e) "Chairperson" shall mean the chairperson of the House of Representatives Rules Drafting Committee, and shall also include the senior member who performs the functions of the chairperson in his absence.
(f) "Speaker" shall mean the Speaker of the House of Representatives.
(g) "Constitution" shall mean the Constitution of Nepal.

3. Term of Office: The term of the committee shall be 14 (fourteen) days from the date of commencement of work.

4. Conduct of Meeting: (1) The committee's meeting shall be conducted by the chairperson. In his absence, the senior member among the present members shall conduct the meeting.
(2) The committee's meeting shall be held on the date, time, and place specified by the chairperson.
(3) The proceedings of the meeting shall commence if a majority of the total number of committee members are present.
(4) While drafting the House of Representatives Rules, the committee may seek opinions and suggestions from the Speaker and Deputy Speaker as needed.
(5) The committee may invite members of the Federal Parliament, former officials, and subject matter experts as needed.
(6) The committee may seek opinions and suggestions from the Secretary General of the Federal Parliament, the Secretary of the House of Representatives, and the Secretariat Secretary, and seek assistance from employees and other individuals during the meeting.
(7) The committee's meeting may be held in camera as needed.

5. Invitation May Be Extended: During the process of drafting the rules, if the committee deems it necessary regarding matters related to the National Assembly Rules, 2075 (Second Amendment), it may invite the party whips in coordination with the Chairperson of the National Assembly.

6. Decision of Meeting: (1) The decision of the committee's meeting shall generally be made by consensus.
(2) If consensus cannot be reached as per Sub-rule (1), the decision shall be made by a majority of the total number of members.
(3) In case of a tie in the committee, the person presiding over the meeting shall cast the deciding vote.
(4) The decision of the meeting shall be authenticated by the chairperson and the secretary.
(5) At least a majority of the members shall sign the committee's report as authentication.

7. Rule Drafting Procedure: (1) The drafting of the House of Representatives Rules shall be based on the Constitution of Nepal, prevailing laws, past House of Representatives rules, and current practices.
(2) Matters agreed upon in the meeting shall be incorporated into the draft in order.
(3) Matters on which consensus cannot be reached shall be resolved through discussion.
(4) For matters where consensus cannot be reached as per Sub-rule (3), the procedure as per Sub-rule (7)(2) shall be followed for decision. However, if any member has a dissenting opinion, such dissenting opinion shall also be included in the report.

8. Committee Report: (1) The committee shall submit its report to the House within its term of office.
(2) The report of the committee shall be presented to the House by the chairperson or, in his absence, by the senior member.

9. Procedure to Be Followed: Matters mentioned in this procedure shall be followed accordingly, and for other matters, the House of Representatives Rules, 2079, shall apply.

10. Amendment: The committee may amend this procedure as needed.

Date of Approval by Committee: 2082 Chaitra 24
