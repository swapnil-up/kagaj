## 1. What this repo is

A toolkit + dataset for turning Nepali government PDFs (Devanagari, often
legacy-font or scanned) into structured, verifiable, provenance-tracked data.
Four independent efforts share OCR/CID infrastructure:

| Effort | What it produces | Maturity |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| **Labour-migration panel** | tidy approvals/deaths/remittances panel | **most mature; the research artifact** |

| Dictionary / DDA drug prices / cidmap | dictionary, drug MRPs, CID mapping tool | shipped / partial |

## 2. TL;DR of what's been built

The headline deliverable is a **verified, reproducible, multi-source panel of
Nepali labour-migration approvals** with an unusually strong integrity contract:
almost every row reconciles exactly against printed subtotals/totals, overlap
between documents is handled explicitly, cross-checked against an independent
open-data digitisation, and packaged with a machine-readable codebook and a
fingerprinted build.

### 3.2 Schema (panel)

One row per `(document × section × name × channel × gender)`. Key fields:
`period_bs`, `period_ad_start/end` (exact ~4-week window), `section`
(`country`|`district`), `row_type` (`detail`|`total`), `channel`, `gender`,
`count` (integer people), `verified`, `source_url`. The canonical layer appends
`name_canonical`, `iso3`, `province`, `fiscal_year`, `channel_canonical`,
`channel_class`, `granularity`, `reconciled_fiscal_year`, merge bookkeeping.

### 3.3 Pipeline (data flow)

`scrape` (build manifest from live DoFE) → `process` (download → extract wide
tables → verify → per-doc CSV → `panel.csv`) → `annex` (2020 report Annex
IX–XIII) → `agencies` (pre-approval/institutional) → `series` (narrative report
matrices → `series.csv`) → `normalize` (canonical entities/channels/years) →
`reconcile` (dedup across granularities → `panel_canonical.csv`) → `verify`
(panel-wide math audit) → `crosscheck` (opendatanepal) → `build` (package +
fingerprint). All driven by `python -m migration_parser.cli <stage>`.

### 3.4 Coverage

- **Approvals (flow, administrative):** live DoFE 2076/77–2082/83 (yearly),
  2082 Shrawan onward (monthly ~4-week windows); report Annexes extend
  destination/district detail back to FY2074/75–2075/76 (2017/18–2018/19);
  narrative reports add year series 2012/13–2016/17 and 2017/18–2023/24.
- **Deaths:** all-cause by year + gender **2008/09–2023/24**; by country,
  district, cause, province (subsets).
- **Return migration:** 2022/23–2023/24 by country/district/gender.
- **Skill/occupation:** 2016/17 and 2022/23–2023/24 crosstabs.
- **Remittance/welfare:** jail/detention, hospitalisation, MRRC, receiving
  households, use, cost; migration cost (USD).
- **Origin geography:** district (2017/18–2018/19 + live yearly), province (2024).

### 3.5 Source families

1. Live DoFE PDFs (`dofe.gov.np`, 35-doc manifest).
2. Narrative reports (6 tracked PDFs: Status 2013/14, 2014/15, 2015/17; Country
   Profile 2019; Migration Report 2020; Migration Report 2024).
3. opendatanepal CSVs (cross-check only).
4. Wayback Machine archive of dead DoFE/FEO reports (**planned**).

### 3.6 Provenance & reproducibility

Every panel row carries `source_url`; every series row carries `report` +
`source_page` + `caption`; `build.json` records the git revision and SHA-256 of
each artifact; `build` exits non-zero if any audit fails or the codebook drifts
from shipped headers. `verified` means *the document passed its own extraction
math audit*, not that a human transcribed it.

## 4. Method (how the integrity claims are earned)

- **Extraction** is vector-text (`PyMuPDF find_tables()`), **no OCR** for
  tabular docs; repairs for dropped/overflowing cells, space/comma thousands,
  `Total` labels in the S.N. column.
- **Per-document verification** (`verify.py`): `M+F=T` per channel; components
  sum to *With*; *With − re-entry = Without*; `Σ detail = printed Total`.
- **Panel-wide verification** (`panel_verify.py`): re-checks the invariants on
  the melted panel independently of extraction. Result: 102,744 rows, only **2
  known failures** (a ±1 worker source typo, Qatar, reproduced not "fixed"), 0
  unexpected.
- **Normalisation**: 170 countries, 79 districts, fiscal years, channel classes
  (`component`/`aggregate`/`measure`), without mutating raw columns.
- **Reconciliation**: never mix monthly vs annual granularity within a FY;
  country vs district tables are one measure along two dimensions; documented
  period override for a misprinted Annex year; **0 cross-document key conflicts**
  and 1,599 intra-document typo merges.
- **Cross-check**: three comparable opendatanepal aggregates agree to
  **< 0.05%** (country, gender, total).

## 5. Strengths (defensible)

1. **Unusual verification density** for a scraped admin dataset; failures are
   *reproduced and documented*, not silently patched — good scientific hygiene.
2. **Explicit overlap/reconciliation model** rather than naive `sum()` — avoids
   the classic double-counting trap when combining monthly + annual + annex.
3. **Provenance at row level** and deterministic, offline-reproducible build.
4. **Multi-source triangulation** (live DoFE + 6 reports + open data + planned
   archive), with a genuine independent cross-check.
5. **Breadth of dimensions** (destination × gender × channel × origin × skill ×
   welfare) far beyond a single approvals table.

## 7. Research angles & threats to validity (economist's lens)

### 7.1 What the data can credibly support
- **Destination-demand shocks → Nepali outflow composition.** Monthly approvals
  by destination are high-frequency (4-week windows) — well suited to event
  studies around Qatar World Cup 2022, Malaysia policy swings, Saudi/GCC oil
  cycles, Israel care-work quota, Japan/Korea EPS expansion, EU care visas.
- **Origin heterogeneity.** District/province cuts (2017/18–2018/19 and 2024)
  for spatial selection / local-labour-market exposure designs.
- **Policy evaluation.** Bilateral MoUs and "free visa/free ticket" cost
  reforms; migration cost (USD), remittance transfer cost (%) are captured.
- **Harm/welfare outcomes.** Deaths (by cause/country/province), return
  migration, detention, hospitalisation, compensation, complaints.

### 7.2 Threats / caveats a reviewer should press on
1. **Approvals ≠ departures ≠ stock.** These are administrative *approval* counts
   (permits issued), not realized migration or migrant stock. Timing, lapsing,
   and re-application create wedge between intention and flow.
2. **Channel redefinition over time** (legalization drives, re-entry vs new,
   G2G vs individual) — the canonical mapping asserts equivalences
   (`legalized_entry`→`legalization` etc.); verify these are legitimate across
   vintages.
3. **Reporting windows are ~4 weeks**, not calendar months; summing monthly docs
   is not a clean calendar series, and overlaps with annual docs are the reason
   for the strict reconciliation rule.
4. **BS fiscal year** (Shrawan–Ashadh) vs AD → all cross-source joins depend on
   the `fiscal_years.json` map; check the monthly→FY derivation.
5. **Denominator/selection:** who applies is endogenous to demand and to agency
   behaviour; the mix of recruiting-agency vs individual channels shifts with
   policy, complicating composition comparisons.
6. **Administrative undercount:** undocumented migration (open India border;
   irregular Gulf routes) is absent by construction.
7. **Cross-source definitional drift** (opendata "new individual", `new_entry`
   intentionally not merged) — do not treat channels as interchangeable without
   the class flags (`component`/`aggregate`/`measure`).
8. **COVID-19 (2020–2021)** is a structural break; monthly coverage before 2082 is
   sparse, so the pre/post design is fragile until the archive backfill lands.
9. **Deaths data** are reported deaths of registered workers (destination
   reporting varies) — likely severe undercount and destination-composition bias.
10. **`verified=True`** means internal arithmetic consistency, **not**
    external validity or freedom from source transcription error.

### 7.3 Research questions worth testing on this data
- Do destination-specific demand shocks (oil price, World Cup, bilateral MoUs)
  shift both the level and the skill/origin composition of approval flows?
- How did the 2017/18 Malaysia cost-reform and the 2019 free-visa push alter
  channel mix (agency vs individual) and origin-district concentration?
- Is the rise of "emerging" destinations (Romania/Croatia/Portugal/UK care,
  Japan/Korea) associated with different gender ratios and death/return rates?
- Does remittance-receiving/use composition respond to destination mix shifts?
- Province/district convergence in migration intensity (σ-convergence) 2017/18 →
  2024.

## 9. Reviewer checklist (what to test, concretely)

1. **Reproduce:** fresh `redbook-env`, run `pytest` (expect 202) and
   `cli build --skip-extract`; confirm audits pass and artifacts regenerate.
2. **Arithmetic spot-checks:** pick 3 destinations in `panel_canonical.csv.gz`;
   verify `Σ component = with`, `M+F=T`, and `Σ detail = printed Total` against
   the source `source_url`.
3. **Overlap test:** sum monthly vs annual doc for one overlapping FY; confirm
   the reconcile rule prevents double counting.
4. **Channel-map audit:** inspect `channels.json` equivalence notes and decide
   whether each asserted mapping is scientifically defensible.
5. **Semantic audit of `series.csv`:** filter by `metric`/`unit`/`year_basis`;
   confirm no accidental summation across incompatible rows (percent vs count;
   deaths vs deaths_by_cause subsets; fiscal vs calendar vs snapshot).
6. **Provenance audit:** sample `source_url`s; confirm the numbers appear in the
   cited PDF page.
7. **Definitional audit:** reconcile the opendata `new_individual` discrepancy;
   decide if it is a research-data problem or an opendata problem.
8. **Staleness audit:** refresh `build.json`/`codebook.json`; fix doc counts.
9. **Coverage proposal:** judge the Archive.org backfill plan (README) and the
   cost/benefit of the remaining narrative-report tables.
